\begin{abstract}
The classical Laguerre--Pólya approach expresses the Riemann Hypothesis (RH)
as an all-orders positivity problem for the Taylor coefficients of Riemann's
$\xi$ function. We present a Lean~4/Mathlib formalization of conditional
reductions within this approach. Given explicitly named external inputs for
the Pólya--Jensen criterion, the Aissen--Schoenberg--Whitney/Edrei
characterization, limit closure, and the converse, the central theorem
identifies RH with total nonnegativity of the sign-normalized coefficient
Toeplitz matrix. This equivalence is classical; the contribution is a checked
dependency structure, together with algebraic coefficient and moment
identities. We distinguish ordinary coefficients $\mu_n$ from the classical
Jensen coefficients $n!\mu_n$, explain why Mathlib's entire pole-subtracted
completed zeta is not itself the correct zero-set target, and record a
separate pseudo-Hermitian eigenvector reduction. Exact rational coefficient
enclosures and FLINT/Arb ball arithmetic certify positivity of $1816$
contiguous Toeplitz minors of orders at most $16$, using coefficients through
index $120$, and strict log-concavity through index $118$. These computations
calibrate the conventions within ranges already covered by classical
positivity results; they do not establish uniformity in the order. A theorem
ledger and replay instructions specify the formal and numerical trust
boundaries. \textbf{No proof of RH is claimed.}
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Riemann Hypothesis; Riemann xi function;
Pólya-frequency sequences; total positivity; Jensen polynomials;
Lean formalization; rigorous ball arithmetic.

\section{Preliminaries and normalization}\label{sec:notation}

\paragraph{The object.} Riemann's entire $\xi$ function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi}
  \xi(s) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,s(s-1)\,\pi^{-s/2}\,\Gamma(s/2)\,\zeta(s)
\end{equation}
is entire, and its zeros are exactly the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$; RH asserts
they all lie on $\re s = \tfrac12$. On the critical line, the real even function
$\Xib(t) = \xi(\tfrac12 + it)$ has a Taylor expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:taylor}
  \Xib(t) \;=\; \sum_{n\ge 0} b_n\, t^{2n},\qquad b_n\in\R,
\end{equation}
and we set the \emph{sign-normalized sequence} $\mu_n := (-1)^n b_n$.

\paragraph{Two classical pillars.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Pólya--Jensen}~\cite{Polya1927,CNV1986}: RH is equivalent to
  hyperbolicity (all roots real) of every Jensen polynomial
  $J^{\mathrm{cl}}_{d,n}(X) = \sum_{k=0}^d \binom{d}{k}\gamma_{n+k} X^k$,
  where $\gamma_n=n!\mu_n$ is the exponential-series coefficient of the
  folded function defined below~\cite{GORTTW}.
  \item \emph{Aissen--Schoenberg--Whitney / Edrei}~\cite{ASW1952,Edrei1953,Karlin1968}:
  a real sequence is a \emph{Pólya-frequency} (PF) sequence---all minors of the
  Toeplitz matrix $[a_{i-j}]$ are nonnegative---iff its generating function is
  $Cz^\ell e^{az}\prod(1+\alpha_i z)/\prod(1-\beta_j z)$ with
  $C,a,\alpha_i,\beta_j\ge 0$, $\ell\ge0$, and
  $\sum_i\alpha_i+\sum_j\beta_j<\infty$. For an entire generating
  function the denominator is absent: this is the nonnegative-coefficient,
  type-I subclass of the Laguerre--Pólya class, not the unrestricted class.
\end{itemize}
Together: \emph{RH $\iff (\mu_n)$ is a Pólya-frequency sequence.} This
consequence is classical; Katkova~\cite{Katkova2007} states it explicitly for
$\xi_1(z)=\xi(\tfrac12+\sqrt z)=\sum_n\mu_n z^n$. She also proves that $(\mu_n)$ is a
Pólya-frequency sequence of every finite order up to an explicit bound, using a
zero-free sector theorem of Schoenberg. Her printed proof gives order $43$, while her
statement gives $44$; we use $43$. Here $\mu_n$ coincides with Katkova's
coefficients and with $\lambda_n=\xi^{(2n)}(\tfrac12)/(2n)!$
of~\cite{LiuMao2026}.

The normalization dictionary in Table~\ref{tab:notation} separates three
coefficient sequences which cannot be interchanged in the Jensen criterion.
Define the folded entire function by its power series, so no choice of a
square-root branch is involved:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:folded}
 F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\mu_n z^n=\sum_{n\ge0}\gamma_n\frac{z^n}{n!},
 \qquad \xi(\tfrac12+w)=F(w^2),\qquad \Xib(t)=F(-t^2).
\end{equation}
Its order is $1/2$. Under RH its zeros are negative, and its genus-zero
product lies in the type-I class above. Conversely, negative real zeros of
$F$ give real zeros of $\Xib$, hence the critical-line zero condition for $\xi$.
The analytic identification and classical characterizations are background
theorems; the formal bridges below carry the corresponding hypotheses.

In particular, the diagnostic polynomial $Q_{d,n}$ is not the classical
Jensen polynomial of $F$. The factorial in $\gamma_n$ changes the
hyperbolicity inequalities. Assertions below about $Q$ are identified
separately and are not substituted into \code{PolyaJensenBridge}.

\paragraph{Pólya's positive kernel.} By Pólya's representation~\cite{Polya1926},
$\Xib(t) = 2\int_0^\infty \Phi(u)\cos(ut)\,du$, in the convention
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 \Phi(u)=\sum_{j\ge1}\left(4\pi^2j^4e^{9u/2}-6\pi j^2e^{5u/2}\right)
               e^{-\pi j^2e^{2u}},\qquad u\ge0.
\end{equation}
Each summand is positive because $2\pi j^2e^{2u}>3$. The double-exponential
decay permits differentiation under the integral at every fixed order, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
  b_n = (-1)^n\,\frac{2 M_n}{(2n)!},\qquad
  M_n := \int_0^\infty \Phi(u)\,u^{2n}\,du > 0,
  \qquad \mu_n = \frac{2 M_n}{(2n)!} > 0 .
\end{equation}
The corresponding generating-function identity is
$F(z)=2\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)\cosh(u\sqrt z)\,du$, with the integrand
understood through its entire power series. The factor $2$ agrees with
the coefficient generator and is essential to the moment normalization.

\section{The target-function correction: $\xi$, not $\Lambda_0$}\label{sec:correction}

We record an error and its repair, because catching it is part of the
contribution. Mathlib's \emph{entire} completed zeta
$\Lambda_0=\texttt{completedRiemannZeta}\textsubscript{\texttt{0}}$ satisfies
$\Lambda = \code{completedRiemannZeta} = \Lambda_0 - 1/s - 1/(1-s)$.

\begin{proposition}[Wrong target]\label{prop:wrong}
The zeros of $\Lambda_0$ are not the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$; in fact
$\Lambda_0$ has no zeros on the critical line at all. At a nontrivial zero
$\rho$ one has $\Lambda(\rho)=0$ but
$\Lambda_0(\rho) = \tfrac1\rho + \tfrac1{1-\rho}=\tfrac1{\rho(1-\rho)}\neq 0$.
On the critical line,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda0-line}
 \Lambda_0(\tfrac12+it)=\frac{1-2\,\Xib(t)}{t^2+\tfrac14}
 \;\ge\;\frac{1-2\,\xi(\tfrac12)}{t^2+\tfrac14}>0
 \qquad(t\in\R),
\end{equation}
with $\xi(\tfrac12)=0.4971207781\ldots<\tfrac12$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\Lambda=\Lambda_0-1/(s(1-s))$ and $\xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\Lambda(s)$,
one has $\Lambda_0(s)=(1-2\xi(s))/(s(1-s))$, and $s(1-s)=t^2+\tfrac14$ at
$s=\tfrac12+it$. Pólya's kernel is positive~\cite{Polya1926}, so
$\Xib(t)=2\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)\cos(tu)\,du$ obeys
$\lvert\Xib(t)\rvert\le2\int_0^\infty\Phi=\Xib(0)=\xi(\tfrac12)$. The certified
coefficient $\mu_0=\xi(\tfrac12)$ (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}) lies in a
ball around $0.4971207781$, strictly below $\tfrac12$.
\end{proof}

Thus the level set $\{\Xib=\tfrac12\}$ named by the naive target is empty on
the real line, and every zero of $\Lambda_0$ lies off the critical line.
Figure~\ref{fig:lambda0} shows the gap. It is small: the minimum of
$1-2\Xib(t)$ is $0.00576$, at $t=0$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{rh_reduction_lambda0.pdf}
  \caption{The Mathlib target trap. (a) $\Xib(t)=\xi(\tfrac12+it)$ crosses
  zero at the zeta zeros (dots) but never reaches the level $\tfrac12$; the
  inset magnifies the gap $\tfrac12-\xi(\tfrac12)\approx0.0029$ at $t=0$.
  (b) $\Lambda_0(\tfrac12+it)$ on a logarithmic scale, with the lower bound

  $\Lambda_0=1/(t^2+\tfrac14)$, which is nonzero. Curves are mpmath
  evaluations at 30 digits.}
  \label{fig:lambda0}
\end{figure}

The remedy is to multiply $\Lambda$ by $s(s-1)/2$ away from $s=0,1$, where
the factor is nonzero, and extend analytically across those two points.
The Lean definition avoids multiplying pointwise pole values by zero:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entire-extension}
 \code{xiCompleted}(s)=\tfrac12+\tfrac12s(s-1)\Lambda_0(s).
\end{equation}
This entire function takes value $1/2$ at $0$ and $1$, and equals the usual
product elsewhere (\code{xiCompleted_eq_product}). We verify that the
corrected slice has the correct zero set:
\begin{theorem}[\code{Xi_eq_zero_iff_riemannZeta}; verified]\label{thm:zeros}
For all $t\in\R$, $\ \Xib(t) = 0 \iff \zeta(\tfrac12 + it) = 0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (mechanized)]
We define $\Xib(t) = -\tfrac{t^2+1/4}{2}\,Z(t)$ where
$Z(t)=\re\Lambda(\tfrac12+it)$ is the verified real critical-line slice. The scalar
$-\tfrac{t^2+1/4}{2}$ is a nonzero real, so $\Xib(t)=0 \iff Z(t)=0 \iff
\Lambda(\tfrac12+it)=0 \iff \zeta(\tfrac12+it)=0$ (the last step using
$\Lambda = \Gamma_{\R}\cdot\zeta$ with $\Gamma_\R(\tfrac12+it)\neq0$). All steps are
checked in Lean.
\end{proof}

All total-positivity, Jensen, and moment results below are stated for this
corrected $\xi$.

\subsection{The Pólya-frequency / Toeplitz route}

\begin{definition}[\code{XiToeplitzTotalPositive}]
The sign-normalized sequence $(\mu_n)$ is \emph{Pólya-frequency total positive} if
every minor of the Toeplitz matrix $[\mu_{i-j}]$ (over all strictly monotone row and
column index selections) is nonnegative.
\end{definition}
Here ``total positive'' follows the historical PF terminology used in the
code; mathematically the condition is \emph{total nonnegativity}. Explicitly,
for all $k\in\N$ and strictly increasing $r,c\colon\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\to\N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-pf}
 \det[\mu_{r_i-c_j}]_{i,j<k}\ge0,
 \qquad \mu_\ell=0\text{ for }\ell<0.
\end{equation}
The contiguous determinants $D_k(m)$ select $r_i=m+i$, $c_j=j$.
Thus full PF implies all contiguous inequalities. A converse from merely
nonnegative contiguous determinants is not proved by the extraction lemmas
in this paper. The later strict-minor closure theorem cited above has
additional hypotheses and must be invoked explicitly when used.

\begin{proposition}[Low-order rungs; verified]\label{prop:rungs}
Under \code{XiToeplitzTotalPositive}: (i) every $1\times1$ minor gives
$\mu_n\ge 0$; (ii) the contiguous $2\times2$ minor equals the Turán determinant,
$\mu_{n+1}^2-\mu_n\mu_{n+2}$, so its nonnegativity is exactly the Turán inequality
$b_n b_{n+2}\le b_{n+1}^2$; (iii) for every order $k$ and offset $m$ the contiguous
minor $\det[\mu_{m+i-j}]_{i,j<k}\ge 0$.
\end{proposition}

We further prove (Lemma \code{xiToeplitzMinor3_sign_eq}) that the order-$3$ signed
minor equals $(-1)^n$ times the unsigned $b$-determinant, exhibiting the correct
alternating-positivity structure; and we document that the Hankel/moment route used
in an earlier draft is the \emph{wrong} machine, since Hankel positivity forces
\emph{log-convexity}, the opposite of the (true) Turán log-concavity.

\subsection{Tightness of the reduction}

Let the three classical external inputs be: \code{XiToeplitzTPToScaledFiniteBridge}
(Edrei--ASW), \code{XiLaguerrePolyaClosureBridge} (LP closure), and
\code{PolyaJensenBridge}; and let \code{XiToeplitzTPFromRH} be the classical
converse (RH $\Rightarrow$ $\xi$ is LP $\Rightarrow$ its coefficients are PF).

\begin{theorem}[\code{xiToeplitzTotalPositive_iff_riemannHypothesis}; verified]\label{thm:tight}
Given the three classical bridges and the classical converse,
\[
  \code{XiToeplitzTotalPositive} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \mathrm{RH}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (mechanized)]
The forward direction composes the ASW/Edrei and closure inputs with the
reverse implication in \code{PolyaJensenBridge}. The reverse direction is
\code{XiToeplitzTPFromRH}. All four are arguments of the theorem; none
is supplied by its proof. Writing $P$ for full PF, $A$ for the scaled
finite-approximation target, and $J$ for classical Jensen hyperbolicity,
the checked composition is
\[
 (P\Rightarrow A),\ (A\Rightarrow J),\ (\mathrm{RH}\Leftrightarrow J),\
 (\mathrm{RH}\Rightarrow P)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 (P\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{RH}).
\]
The finite-approximation witness requires locally uniform convergence on
$\C$, in addition to convergence on $\R$; pointwise real convergence alone
does not meet this interface.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The honesty linchpin]
Theorem~\ref{thm:tight} is the classical equivalence~\cite{Polya1927,ASW1952,Edrei1953,Katkova2007}
in machine-checked form. Its value is not mathematical novelty. It mechanically pins down
the one hypothesis that is not classical, and shows that this input is \emph{equivalent} to
RH. Hence declaring it an axiom is logically identical to
\texttt{axiom rh : RiemannHypothesis}: it does not constitute a proof, and
\texttt{\#print axioms} would expose a newly declared axiom. An equivalent
criterion can guide a proof, but a proof still has to establish that criterion
without assuming RH. The axiom footprint checks dependencies of the
conditional theorem; it does not discharge its explicit hypotheses.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The complete moment-determinant ladder}

Under Pólya's representation (the named external input \code{XiMomentKernelRep},
encoding~\eqref{eq:moments}), the PF condition transfers to explicit moment
determinants at \emph{every} order.

\begin{theorem}[\code{xiToeplitzContigMinor_eq_of_kernelRep}; verified]\label{thm:ladder}
For all $k,m\in\N$,
\[
  \det\bigl[\mu_{m+i-j}\bigr]_{i,j<k}
  \;=\; 2^{k}\cdot \det\!\left[\frac{M_{m+i-j}}{(2(m+i-j))!}\right]_{i,j<k}.
\]
Every entry with negative subscript on the right is defined to be zero.
Consequently (\code{xiToeplitzContigMinor_nonneg_iff_moment_of_kernelRep}),
for each $k,m$, nonnegativity of this contiguous coefficient determinant is
equivalent to nonnegativity of the corresponding weighted moment determinant.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (mechanized)]
Each entry of the signed Toeplitz matrix equals $2$ times the corresponding
factorial-weighted moment entry (by~\eqref{eq:moments}); the determinant is then
$2^k$ times the moment determinant by multilinearity (\texttt{Matrix.det\_smul},
$\#\,\mathrm{Fin}\,k=k$). Positivity of $2^k$ gives the equivalence.
\end{proof}
The displayed Lean theorem concerns contiguous blocks. The same entrywise
scaling gives an ordinary mathematical identity for arbitrary increasing
row and column selections, with the same factor $2^k$. Only when
\emph{all} such selections are quantified does this become a full PF
reformulation. Positivity of the kernel gives $M_n>0$ and positive Hankel
moment matrices; it does not by itself prove these weighted Toeplitz inequalities.

The order-$2$ mechanism is quantified by the \emph{slack factor}
$s_n = \dfrac{(2n+3)(2n+4)}{(2n+1)(2n+2)}$, for which we verify $s_n>1$
(\code{one_lt_slackFactor}) and $s_{n+1}<s_n$ (\code{slackFactor_succ_lt}); thus
$s_n\downarrow 1$. To prevent confusing coefficient ratios with moment
ratios, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratios}
 H_n=\frac{M_{n+1}^2}{M_nM_{n+2}},\qquad
 R_n=\frac{\mu_{n+1}^2}{\mu_n\mu_{n+2}}=s_n H_n.
\end{equation}
Moment log-convexity gives $H_n\le1$, while coefficient log-concavity is
$R_n\ge1$, equivalently $H_n\ge1/s_n$. These are compatible statements.
The elementary slack gap is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slack-gap}
 1-\frac1{s_n}=\frac{8n+10}{(2n+3)(2n+4)}\sim\frac2n.
\end{equation}
The threshold $1/s_n$ applies to $H_n$, not to $R_n$.

\subsection{Order two: a diagnostic quadratic and the classical normalization}

\begin{theorem}[\code{jensenPoly_two_hyperbolic_of_turan}; verified]\label{thm:jensen2}
If $b_{n+2}\neq 0$ and the Turán inequality $b_n b_{n+2}\le b_{n+1}^2$ holds, then
the degree-$2$ diagnostic polynomial $Q_{2,n}$ is hyperbolic (all complex roots real).
Hence (\code{allJensenTwo_hyperbolic_of_turan2All}) if all coefficients are nonzero
and all Turán inequalities hold, every $Q_{2,n}$ is hyperbolic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (mechanized)]
$Q_{2,n}$ maps to $b_{n+2}z^2 + 2b_{n+1}z + b_n$ over $\C$. Completing the square,
$(b_{n+2}z + b_{n+1})^2 = b_{n+1}^2 - b_n b_{n+2} =: D \ge 0$ at any root. The
auxiliary lemma \code{im_zero_of_sq_eq_nonneg_real} ($w^2 = D\in\R_{\ge0}
\Rightarrow \im w = 0$) forces $b_{n+2}z + b_{n+1}\in\R$, hence (as $b_{n+2}\neq0$
real) $\im z = 0$. No square roots and no zero-locus input are used.
\end{proof}
Nondegeneracy $b_{n+2}\neq0$ is supplied by~\eqref{eq:moments} ($M_n>0$).
The analytic input is classical. Csordas, Norfolk and Varga~\cite{CNV1986} proved
Pólya's conjectured Turán inequalities for the $\xi$ coefficients. Dimitrov and
Lucas~\cite{DimitrovLucas2011} proved the higher-order (degree-$3$) Turán
inequalities without assuming RH. Theorem~\ref{thm:jensen2} is the formal bridge
for the diagnostic normalization, not a new inequality or a proof of the
classical Jensen criterion. For that criterion the correct quadratic condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical-quadratic}
 \gamma_{n+1}^2\ge\gamma_n\gamma_{n+2}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mu_{n+1}^2\ge\frac{n+2}{n+1}\mu_n\mu_{n+2}.
\end{equation}
It is stronger than plain log-concavity of $\mu$. The classical results on
degrees two and three concern the factorial-normalized polynomials
$J^{\mathrm{cl}}_{d,n}$~\cite{GORTTW}; the displayed Lean diagnostic theorem
does not identify those two families.

\subsection{An independent operator-positivity route}

The naive Hilbert--Pólya operator must be self-adjoint for the ambient inner
product---the obstruction. The pseudo-Hermitian viewpoint~\cite{Mostafazadeh2002,BBM2017}
keeps only a \emph{positive metric}.

\begin{definition}[\code{PseudoHermitianForm}]
For a $\C$-linear $T$ on a $\C$-vector space $E$, the formal interface is a map
$\eta\colon E\times E\to\C$ with
$\eta(cx,y)=c\eta(x,y)$, $\eta(x,cy)=\bar c\eta(x,y)$,
$\eta(Tx,y)=\eta(x,Ty)$, and $\eta(x,x)\neq0$ for every $x\neq0$.
A positive-definite sesquilinear form supplies these properties. The Lean
structure records only these scalar laws, invariance, and diagonal
nonvanishing; it does not require additivity or Hermitian symmetry.
Diagonal nonvanishing is stronger than the usual nondegeneracy of a form.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\code{PseudoHermitianForm.eigenvalue_conj_eq} and
\code{riemannHypothesis_of_pseudoHermitianWitness}; verified]\label{thm:ph}
An $\eta$-self-adjoint operator has real eigenvalues. Consequently, if the
every critical-strip zero $s$ has a nonzero eigenvector with eigenvalue
$\lambda(s) = -i\,(s-\tfrac12)$ (real iff $\re s=\tfrac12$), then RH holds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (mechanized)]
For an eigenpair $Tv=\lambda v$, $v\neq0$:
$\lambda\,\eta(v,v)=\eta(Tv,v)=\eta(v,Tv)=\bar\lambda\,\eta(v,v)$, and $\eta(v,v)\neq0$, so
$\lambda=\bar\lambda$. The proof uses only the form laws---no inner product,
completeness or boundedness. It is a separate conditional implication;
logical independence from other RH criteria is not asserted. The witness
requires a zero-to-eigenvector map, not equality with the full operator
spectrum. No construction of such a witness is supplied. Closedness, domains
and continuous spectrum would be additional obligations for an unbounded
Hilbert-space realization.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Certified claims.} The script \code{paper/certify_reduction_minors.py}
works at $8192$ bits. It certifies in ball arithmetic, so that each output ball lies
strictly in $(0,\infty)$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] $\mu_n>0$ for $0\le n\le120$;
  \item[(ii)] strict log-concavity $\mu_{n+1}^2>\mu_n\mu_{n+2}$ for $0\le n\le118$;
  \item[(iii)] every contiguous zero-extended Toeplitz minor
  $\det[\mu_{m+i-j}]_{i,j<k}$ with $1\le k\le16$ and $m+k-1\le120$ is strictly
  positive. These are $1816$ minors, and the largest relative radius of a determinant
  ball is $6.5\times10^{-669}$, at $k=16$, $m=105$.
\end{enumerate}
The certificate output, including the input hash, is stored in

together with the classical identities behind the enclosures. These are not Lean
certificates. The June draft also plotted floating-point roots for $d\le6$ and
$n\le114$. The source script uses $Q_{d,n}$, not $J^{\mathrm{cl}}_{d,n}$;
that uncertified diagnostic plot is omitted from this revision and is not
used as evidence for the classical Jensen criterion.

\paragraph{What the checks do and do not show.} Nonnegativity in (iii) is already a
theorem: Katkova's finite-order result covers every minor of order $\le43$, at
every index~\cite{Katkova2007}. Claim~(ii) is a special case of the infinite
log-concavity of $(\mu_n)$ proved in~\cite{LiuMao2026}. That proof is a preprint and is
computer-assisted. For Jensen polynomials, hyperbolicity is known for $d\le3$ at
every shift~\cite{CNV1986,DimitrovLucas2011}, and for every fixed $d$ at all
sufficiently large shifts~\cite{GORZ2019,GORTTW}. The computations above are
therefore calibration of conventions and margins: strictness with explicit
error control in a finite range. They are not discoveries of previously unknown
positivity. The figures in this revision are regenerated directly from the certified
coefficient artifact using the corrected normalization dictionary.

The classical Jensen baseline extends far beyond degree three: Corollary~1.3
of~\cite{GORTTW}, using a finite verification of low zeros, gives
hyperbolicity for all shifts when $d\le9.36\times10^{20}$. This is a
fixed-degree result for $J^{\mathrm{cl}}_{d,n}$, not an all-degrees theorem
and not a result about the unnormalized diagnostic $Q_{d,n}$.

\section{The open frontier}\label{sec:frontier}

The reductions convert ``zeros on a line'' into positivity statements about the
explicit positive kernel $\Phi$ and an explicit operator metric---objects amenable
to tools foreign to zero-counting.
\begin{description}
  \item[A. Uniform total positivity.] Prove~\eqref{eq:full-pf} for all orders
  and arbitrary increasing row and column selections. This is RH-equivalent,
  given the classical inputs of Theorem~\ref{thm:tight}. Proving strict
  contiguous positivity for every $k,m$, followed by an applicable closure
  theorem, is one possible sufficient route. Every \emph{fixed} order up to
  $43$ is classical~\cite{Katkova2007}. A recent preprint claims strict positivity
  of every contiguous minor of order $r\ge2$ at all shifts $m\ge10^{18}r^3$, using
  certified saddle-point estimates~\cite{Michalowski2026wedge}. The open content is
  therefore the complementary region of unbounded order and moderate shift.
  In that preprint's convention $a_n=\mu_n/(8\cdot4^n)$, so its transposed
  contiguous determinant is $8^{-r}4^{-mr}D_r(m)$; the positive scaling
  preserves signs. This normalization transfer does not verify the preprint's
  analytic estimates. The continuous
  de~Bruijn--Newman kernel is not PF$_5$~\cite{Michalowski2026pf5}, so a
  continuous-kernel total-positivity shortcut is unavailable. Classical tools are
  Karlin total positivity~\cite{Karlin1968}, Schoenberg Pólya-frequency functions, and
  variation-diminishing criteria for the representation
  $F(z)=2\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)\cosh(u\sqrt z)\,du$.
  \item[B. Quantitative margins.] Obtain effective bounds on $R_n-1$ and
  the corresponding moment margin $H_n-1/s_n=(R_n-1)/s_n$.
  The elementary slack gap is of order $1/n$, as in~\eqref{eq:slack-gap}.
  Such fixed-order estimates alone cannot establish full PF.
  Tools: the Griffin--Ono--Rolen--Zagier Hermite asymptotics~\cite{GORZ2019} and
  their effective form~\cite{GORTTW}. Farmer argues that Jensen-polynomial
  asymptotics alone are not a plausible route to RH~\cite{Farmer2022}.
  \item[C. $\mu$ log-concavity (settled).] The June draft listed
  $\mu_{n+1}^2>\mu_n\mu_{n+2}$ as open. By~\eqref{eq:moments} it is equivalent to
  $M_{n+1}^2/(M_nM_{n+2})>1/s_n=\frac{(2n+1)(2n+2)}{(2n+3)(2n+4)}$. The Turán
  inequalities of Csordas--Norfolk--Varga~\cite{CNV1986} give
  $M_{n+1}^2>\frac{2n+1}{2n+3}M_nM_{n+2}$, which is stronger. A recent preprint proves much more,
  strict \emph{infinite} log-concavity of $(\mu_n)$~\cite{LiuMao2026}. Like
  Jensen asymptotics, this is a consequence of RH and does not imply it.
  \item[D. Operator witness.] Construct $(E,T,\eta)$ and a nonzero
  eigenvector for every critical-strip zero with eigenvalue $-i(s-1/2)$;
  a formal operator expression alone does not supply this witness
  (cf.~\cite{BBM2017}).
\end{description}

The de Bruijn--Newman programme provides a dynamical handle on the same
object. Writing its constant as $\Lambda_{\mathrm{DN}}$ to distinguish it
from completed zeta, the established bounds are
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{DN}}\ge0$~\cite{RodgersTao2020} and
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{DN}}\le0.2$~\cite{Polymath2019}, while
RH $\iff\Lambda_{\mathrm{DN}}\le0$.

\begin{remark}[A conditional finite-order improvement]\label{rem:qrh}
Katkova's order $43$ uses two inputs. The first is that nontrivial zeros satisfy
$|\re\rho-\tfrac12|\le\tfrac12$. The second is that no zero has
$|\im\rho|<\gamma_1=14.1347\ldots$. In the variable $z=(s-\tfrac12)^2$, a zero
$\tfrac12+x+i\gamma$ lies at angle $2\arctan(|x|/\gamma)$ from the negative axis.
Schoenberg's sector theorem yields PF$_m$ when this angle is at most $\pi/(m+1)$.
A recent manuscript claims that $\zeta(s)\neq0$ for $\re s>7/8$~\cite{OpenAI2026QRH}.
The external collection advertises a Lean formalization, but neither that
formalization nor its analytic proof is audited here. It is treated only as
an external claim, not as a verified input to this manuscript. If the claim holds, then
$|x|\le\tfrac38$, the maximal angle drops from $0.07072$ to $0.05305$, and the
same argument gives PF$_{58}$ in place of PF$_{43}$. This is a constant improvement only.
Verification of RH to a specified finite height can improve the resulting
finite-order bound; a single finite verification does not establish all orders. The remark is not used elsewhere in this paper.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A Davenport--Heilbronn sector control}\label{sec:dh-sector-control}

To test whether the sufficient sector rank predicts the failure rank of the
full coefficient sequence, use the conductor-five Davenport--Heilbronn
function~\cite{BombieriGhosh2011}, with its entire odd completion
\[
 \Lambda_H(s)=(5/\pi)^{s/2}\Gamma((s+1)/2)H(s),\qquad
 F_H(w)=\Lambda_H(1/2+\sqrt w)/\Lambda_H(1/2).
\]
The ordinary coefficients of $F_H$, rather than their factorial multiples,
are used below. These are new computer-assisted results in the working tree,
separate from the pinned Lean results described in the next section.

An absolute Dirichlet-tail bound below $0.737591$ at $\re s=3/2$ and the
completed functional equation give $|\re\rho-1/2|<1$. An Arb
argument-principle computation counts $249$ zeros in
$[-1/2,3/2]+i(0,340)$; $235$ critical-line sign brackets and seven reflected
pairs of simple Rouch\'e disks account for the entire count. Comparing all
seven right-hand disks and the higher-zero bound $2\arctan(1/340)$ proves
that $0.80851718\ldots+85.69934849\ldots i$ has the steepest folded angle
$\phi$, with
\[
 436<\pi/\phi=436.3348656\ldots<437.
\]
The genus-zero product and Schoenberg's sufficient sector theorem
therefore give PF$_{435}$. For the isolated quadratic factor, the
shift-one rank-$k$ minor is
$U_k(\cos\phi)=\sin((k+1)\phi)/\sin\phi$, first negative at $k=436$.

Nevertheless every one of the $18\,291$ full-function solid minors with
$430\le r\le520$ and $0\le m\le200$ is strictly positive at $32\,768$ bits,
with no unresolved signs. The complete condensation sign stream is
replayed, and $24$ selected high-rank minors are checked independently
using complementary determinants. Thus the isolated factor's sharp rank
does not predict a failure on this full-function grid. Neither the
globally first negative minor nor PF$_{520}$ follows from a bounded
solid-minor scan. Exact balls, analytic dependencies, source hashes and
reproduction instructions are in
\code{research/prime\_spectral/RESEARCH\_ROUND\_2026\_10\_12.md}.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item No proof of RH: the PF capstone still requires the all-orders
  positivity input, as well as its named classical bridges. The operator
  reduction requires the zero-indexed eigenvector witness. No combination
  of the classical background theorems alone supplies these inputs.
  \item The numerics are finite. Ball arithmetic certifies positivity only for $n\le120$
  and Toeplitz order $k\le16$, under the FLINT/Arb trust boundary. The legacy diagnostic
  root observations for $d\le6$ are floating-point only and are omitted. None of this can establish the
  uniform ($k,n\to\infty$) statement that is RH-equivalent, and the certified range
  lies inside what known theorems already cover.
  \item The pseudo-Hermitian existence claim is entirely open and unevidenced.
  \item Equivalence-chain reformulations (e.g.\ ``$\le 1$ zero per height'') are
  correct but RH-equivalent, hence reorganization rather than progress.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Classical background.} Classical sources are Pólya~\cite{Polya1926,Polya1927},
Aissen--Schoenberg--Whitney~\cite{ASW1952}, Edrei~\cite{Edrei1953} and Karlin~\cite{Karlin1968}.
For the $\xi$ coefficients specifically, Katkova~\cite{Katkova2007} gives the
Pólya-frequency formulation and finite-order positivity. The Turán and higher-order Turán
inequalities are due to Csordas--Norfolk--Varga~\cite{CNV1986} and
Dimitrov--Lucas~\cite{DimitrovLucas2011}.

\paragraph{Jensen polynomials and heat flow.} Jensen-polynomial asymptotics are developed
in~\cite{GORZ2019,GORTTW}, with the critique of~\cite{Farmer2022}. On the de~Bruijn--Newman
constant see~\cite{RodgersTao2020,Polymath2019}.

\paragraph{Recent work on the coefficients and kernel.} Three 2026 preprints concern
these objects: a tail wedge for contiguous Toeplitz minors~\cite{Michalowski2026wedge},
certified failure of PF$_5$ for the continuous kernel~\cite{Michalowski2026pf5}, and infinite
log-concavity~\cite{LiuMao2026}. A claimed zero-free half-plane $\re s>7/8$~\cite{OpenAI2026QRH}
bears on finite-order constants only (Remark~\ref{rem:qrh}).

\paragraph{Operators and formalization.} For the pseudo-Hermitian viewpoint see
Mostafazadeh~\cite{Mostafazadeh2002} and Bender--Brody--Müller~\cite{BBM2017}. The formal
foundations are Mathlib~\cite{Mathlib2020} and its zeta and $L$-function
library~\cite{LoefflerStoll2025}. Spectral and adelic perspectives (Berry--Keating, Connes)
and the Weil and Li positivity criteria are classical antecedents of the operator and
positivity viewpoints used here.

\section{Conclusion}
The checked contribution is a conditional deduction architecture: full PF
positivity and named analytic bridges imply RH, and a named converse makes
that reduction tight. The accompanying coefficient identities distinguish
ordinary Taylor coefficients, factorial-normalized Jensen coefficients and
kernel moments. The operator theorem supplies a separate eigenvector-based
implication. The finite Arb certificates validate conventions and strictness
in the stated bounded domain.

Further work has three distinct obligations: formalize the classical
analytic bridges with their complete hypotheses; establish all-orders
positivity rather than a fixed-order or large-shift fragment; and obtain
independent review of the analytic identification and numerical enclosure
formulas. None is discharged by the conditional theorem or finite data.
