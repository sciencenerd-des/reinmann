\begin{abstract}
We deform the Taylor coefficients of Riemann's $\Xi$-function under the de
Bruijn--Newman heat flow. We follow the evolution of the degree-two Jensen
(Tur\'an) discriminants of the factorial-normalized coefficients. The main
result is an exact transport identity:
$\partial_t\log(\bar\Delta(n,t)+1)=-\Delta^2\rho_n(t)$. Here $\bar\Delta$ is
the normalized Tur\'an discriminant and $\rho_n=M_{n+1}/M_n$ is the ratio of
consecutive kernel moments. We also record a normalization trap. For the raw
kernel moments the quadratic discriminant is negative at every index and
every time, by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality. A weighted logarithmic
``entropy'' of the Tur\'an discriminants is well defined when they are
positive, which at $t=0$ is a classical theorem, and when they decay at most
polynomially. Its derivative follows from the transport identity. Degree two
alone cannot reach the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), and we state precisely what
additional input would be needed.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Correction notice.} This version replaces the June 2026 draft.
That draft built its Jensen polynomials from raw kernel moments $b_m$,
treating them as if they were Taylor coefficients of $\Xi$. For those
moments the quadratic discriminant is negative for every $n$ and $t$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:trap}), so its entropy functional was undefined. The
draft also substituted $b_{n+1}/b_n=-\rho_n$ although $b_m>0$, which reversed
the sign of its transport law. It further asserted that monotonicity of the
functional is equivalent to RH, and that ``Dyson stiffness'' of GUE spacings
protects that monotonicity. Both assertions are withdrawn. The first fails
logically (Section~\ref{sec:scope}); the second is a statistical heuristic,
not an argument. The draft's claim that the GORZ threshold grows like
$e^{Cd}$ was unsupported and has been removed.

\section{Introduction}
RH is equivalent to the hyperbolicity (all roots real) of every Jensen
polynomial attached to the factorial-normalized Taylor coefficients of
$\Xi$~\cite{Polya1927,Jensen1913}. Griffin, Ono, Rolen and Zagier proved
that for each fixed degree $d$ these polynomials are hyperbolic for all
sufficiently large shifts $n$~\cite{GORZ2019}. Degree two, the Tur\'an
inequalities, holds for every shift~\cite{CNV1986}. Degree three is due to
Dimitrov and Lucas~\cite{DimitrovLucas2011}.

We study how these discriminants move under the de Bruijn--Newman heat
flow~\cite{deBruijn1950,Newman1976}. Increasing the flow time makes zeros
real; RH is the statement that the de Bruijn--Newman constant satisfies
$\Lambda\le0$. It is known that $0\le\Lambda\le0.2$
\cite{RodgersTao2020,Polymath2019,PlattTrudgian2021}. Our contribution is
modest and exact: a closed transport law for the order-two discriminants, a
correct normalization, and a sharp statement of what such a law can and
cannot give.

\section{The heat flow and its coefficients}
We use de Bruijn's normalization, in which $\Lambda$ is defined. Let
\begin{equation}
\Phi(u)=\sum_{k\ge1}\bigl(2\pi^2k^4e^{9u}-3\pi k^2e^{5u}\bigr)
       \exp\bigl(-\pi k^2e^{4u}\bigr),\qquad u\ge0,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
H_t(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,\qquad
H_0(z)=\tfrac18\,\Xi(z/2).
\end{equation}
The kernel is positive~\cite{Polya1926} and decays double-exponentially, so
every moment
\begin{equation}
M_m(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\,u^{2m}\,du>0
\end{equation}
is finite for every real $t$. Expanding the cosine,
\[
H_t(z)=\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{(-1)^mM_m(t)}{(2m)!}z^{2m}=F_t(-z^2),\qquad
F_t(x)=\sum_{m\ge0}\mu_m(t)x^m,\quad \mu_m=\frac{M_m}{(2m)!}>0 .
\]
Put $\gamma_m=m!\,\mu_m$ and define the Jensen polynomials
$J^{d,n}_t(X)=\sum_{j=0}^d\binom dj\gamma_{n+j}(t)X^j$. All zeros of $H_t$
are real exactly when all zeros of $F_t$ are real (and then negative, since
$\mu_m>0$). By the Jensen--Pólya theory~\cite{Polya1927,Jensen1913}, this
holds exactly when every $J^{d,n}_t$ is hyperbolic. By the definition of
$\Lambda$, it holds exactly when $t\ge\Lambda$.

\begin{lemma}[Moment flow]\label{lem:moment}
$\partial_tM_m(t)=M_{m+1}(t)$ for all $m\ge0$ and $t\in\mathbb R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate under the integral; double-exponential decay of $\Phi$
justifies dominated convergence locally uniformly in $t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient flow]\label{lem:gamma}
$\partial_t\gamma_m=2(2m+1)\,\gamma_{m+1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:moment}, $\partial_t\gamma_m=m!\,M_{m+1}/(2m)!$, while
$\gamma_{m+1}=(m+1)!\,M_{m+1}/(2m+2)!$. Their ratio is
$(2m+2)(2m+1)/(m+1)=2(2m+1)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Two discriminants, one of them always negative}

\begin{proposition}[The raw-moment trap]\label{prop:trap}
For every $n\ge0$ and $t\in\mathbb R$, $M_{n+1}(t)^2<M_n(t)M_{n+2}(t)$.
Consequently the quadratic $M_n+2M_{n+1}X+M_{n+2}X^2$ has two nonreal roots
for every $n$ and $t$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality to $u^{2n}$ and $u^{2n+4}$ in
$L^2(e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\,du)$. Equality would force $u^2$ to be constant on the
support of $\Phi$, which is $[0,\infty)$.
\end{proof}

The correct object uses the factorial normalization. Define the
\emph{normalized Tur\'an discriminant}
\begin{equation}
\bar\Delta(n,t)=\frac{\gamma_{n+1}(t)^2}{\gamma_n(t)\gamma_{n+2}(t)}-1,
\qquad
\operatorname{disc}J^{2,n}_t=4\gamma_n\gamma_{n+2}\,\bar\Delta(n,t).
\end{equation}
Thus $J^{2,n}_t$ is hyperbolic if and only if $\bar\Delta(n,t)\ge0$. A direct
computation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corridor}
\bar\Delta(n,t)+1=\frac{2n+3}{2n+1}\cdot\frac{M_{n+1}^2}{M_nM_{n+2}} ,
\end{equation}
so by Proposition~\ref{prop:trap} the Tur\'an condition is the band
$\frac{2n+1}{2n+3}\le M_{n+1}^2/(M_nM_{n+2})<1$, of width $2/(2n+3)$. In
particular $-1<\bar\Delta(n,t)<\frac{2}{2n+1}\le2$. Two facts are known:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\bar\Delta(n,0)>0$ for every $n\ge0$. These are the Tur\'an
inequalities for $\Xi$, proved by Csordas, Norfolk and Varga~\cite{CNV1986}.
\item $\bar\Delta(n,t)\ge0$ for every $n$ when $t\ge\Lambda$, since then
$F_t$ lies in the Laguerre--Pólya class.
\end{itemize}

\section{The transport identity}

\begin{theorem}[Tur\'an transport]\label{thm:transport}
Let $\rho_n(t)=M_{n+1}(t)/M_n(t)$ and
$\Delta^2\rho_n=\rho_{n+2}-2\rho_{n+1}+\rho_n$. For all $n\ge0$ and
$t\in\mathbb R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\bigl(\bar\Delta(n,t)+1\bigr)=-\Delta^2\rho_n(t),
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\frac{\partial\bar\Delta}{\partial t}=-(\bar\Delta+1)\,\Delta^2\rho_n .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:moment},
$\partial_t\rho_n=(M_{n+2}M_n-M_{n+1}^2)/M_n^2=\rho_n(\rho_{n+1}-\rho_n)$,
so $\partial_t\log\rho_n=\rho_{n+1}-\rho_n$. By~\eqref{eq:corridor},
$\log(\bar\Delta+1)=\log\frac{2n+3}{2n+1}+\log\rho_n-\log\rho_{n+1}$, whose
first term is independent of $t$. Differentiating,
$(\rho_{n+1}-\rho_n)-(\rho_{n+2}-\rho_{n+1})=-\Delta^2\rho_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The identity concerns the kernel moments only, so it holds for any positive
kernel with finite moments, not just for $\Xi$. Its value is that it turns
the time evolution of every Tur\'an discriminant into a convexity statement
about a single sequence. Central finite differences at step $10^{-8}$,
computed with 40-digit quadrature, agree with~\eqref{eq:transport} to a
relative error below $10^{-19}$ for $0\le n\le5$ at $t=0$.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Observation.} Using midpoints of certified enclosures of the
first 201 coefficients, $\Delta^2\rho_n(0)<0$ for every $0\le n\le196$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}, each of these Tur\'an discriminants is
increasing in $t$ at $t=0$.

\begin{conjecture}\label{q:concave}
Is $\Delta^2\rho_n(t)<0$ for all $n\ge0$ and all $t\ge0$? Equivalently, is
the moment-ratio sequence of $e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)$ strictly concave? An
affirmative answer would make every Tur\'an ratio nondecreasing along the
forward flow. It concerns degree two only, so it would not bear on RH.
\end{conjecture}

\section{The entropy functional, correctly defined}

\begin{definition}
Let $T^+=\{t:\bar\Delta(n,t)>0\ \text{for all}\ n\}$. For $\alpha>0$ and
$t\in T^+$, put
$\displaystyle\mathcal H_\alpha(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-\alpha n}\log\bar\Delta(n,t)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Convergence]\label{prop:conv}
Let $t\in T^+$, and suppose $\bar\Delta(n,t)\ge c\,(n+1)^{-K}$ for some
$c,K>0$ and all $n$. Then the series for $\mathcal H_\alpha(t)$ converges
absolutely.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From $\bar\Delta<2$ and the hypothesis,
$\lvert\log\bar\Delta(n,t)\rvert\le\log2+\lvert\log c\rvert+K\log(n+1)$.
The series $\sum e^{-\alpha n}\log(n+1)$ converges.
\end{proof}

At $t=0$ the positivity hypothesis is the Csordas--Norfolk--Varga theorem.
For the polynomial lower bound we have only evidence:
$n\,\bar\Delta(n,0)$ increases from $0.28$ at $n=10$ to $0.57$ at $n=197$.
Effective forms of the GORZ asymptotics should prove a bound of the form
$\bar\Delta(n,0)\ge c/(n+1)$; we have not carried this out. On any
interval of $T^+$ where the lower bound holds uniformly and the
differentiated series converges locally uniformly,
Theorem~\ref{thm:transport} gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\mathcal H_\alpha}{dt}
=-\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-\alpha n}\,\frac{\bar\Delta(n,t)+1}{\bar\Delta(n,t)}\,
\Delta^2\rho_n(t).
\end{equation}

\section{What this framework can and cannot give}\label{sec:scope}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Degree two is not RH-strength.} At $t=0$ every Tur\'an
discriminant is already positive by~\cite{CNV1986}. A statement about
$\mathcal H_\alpha$ alone therefore adds nothing toward RH, which needs all
degrees $d$ simultaneously.
\item \textbf{The direction of monotonicity.} If
$d\mathcal H_\alpha/dt\ge0$ on $[0,\infty)$, integration bounds
$\mathcal H_\alpha(0)$ from \emph{above} by later values. It cannot keep
$\mathcal H_\alpha(0)$ away from $-\infty$. Transporting positivity from
$t\ge\Lambda$, where it holds, back to $t=0$ needs backward-in-time
control. That is either $d\mathcal H/dt\le0$ on $[0,\Lambda]$, or a bound
on $\lvert d\mathcal H/dt\rvert$ there. This is exactly as hard as the
original problem unless it can be made uniform in $d$.
\item \textbf{The open input.} An RH-relevant version would need transport
identities for the degree-$d$ discriminants of $J^{d,n}_t$, together with
backward control uniform in $d$. We do not know such identities for
$d\ge3$; finding them is the natural next step.
\item \textbf{Withdrawn heuristics.} Statistical properties of zeta zeros
(pair correlation, GUE spacing) are averaged statements. They give no
deterministic sign information about any individual
$\Delta^2\rho_n(t)$, and they are not used here.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
Corrected, the Hyperbolic Entropic Flow is a bookkeeping device for an exact
identity: under the de Bruijn--Newman flow, the logarithm of each
factorial-normalized Tur\'an ratio moves at minus the second difference of
the kernel moment ratios. The identity, its numerical confirmation and the
raw-moment trap are solid. The observed concavity of moment ratios
(Question~\ref{q:concave}) is a clean, kernel-only conjecture. Extending the
transport law to higher-degree discriminants is the open problem that would
connect this framework to RH. Nothing here proves RH or reduces it to a
statement shown to be more tractable.
