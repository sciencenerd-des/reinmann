\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{We do not prove the Riemann Hypothesis.}  We present a
Lean~4 / Mathlib formalization (76 modules, $\approx 29{,}000$ lines,
122 audited theorems depending only on Lean's three foundational axioms, with
no \texttt{sorry} and no project axioms) that reduces RH to explicitly named
open positivity inputs along two independent, \emph{exact-ready} routes, and
that isolates---by proof and by machine-checked counterexample---precisely
which new mathematics remains.

The formal core is a machine-checked Fekete-type total positivity criterion:
for the lower-triangular Toeplitz
matrix of the signed Taylor coefficients of Riemann's $\Xi$-function,
\emph{strict positivity of all contiguous minors implies full total
positivity}.  The proof mechanizes a generic Grassmann syzygy and a
three-term gap-shrinking identity valid at every matrix order, an all-orders
double induction (on order and row dispersion), a dominance dichotomy whose
vanishing half is a permutation-pigeonhole argument, and a Toeplitz reversal
symmetry.  Combined with the classical scaffolding
(Aissen--Schoenberg--Whitney/Edrei, Laguerre--P\'olya closure,
P\'olya--Jensen), this yields a minimal named conditional reduction: RH
follows from the strict contiguous ladder alone,
with no initial-column criterion and no kernel representation.

We further formalize a bounded-degree hyperbolic-limit theorem (a
finite-dimensional Hurwitz-type statement with a fully elementary proof), a
receptor architecture that lets each future ingredient---an explicit
remainder bound, a certified prefix, or the general same-sign
Schur--Szeg\H{o} composition theorem---close its route by a single
\texttt{exact}, and a machine-checked counterexample showing that the known
second- and third-order Tur\'an inequalities for the $\Xi$ coefficients
cannot, at the sequence level, close even the order-$3$ rung of the ladder.
We conclude with a research map of the new mathematics the architecture is
ready to receive.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Riemann hypothesis; P\'olya frequency sequences;
Toeplitz total positivity; Jensen polynomials; formal mathematics; Lean~4.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) \cite{Riemann1859} admits a classical
reformulation in the language of total positivity: writing
$\Xi(t) = \sum_{n \ge 0} b_n t^{2n}$ for the Taylor expansion of Riemann's
$\Xi$-function and $\mu_n = (-1)^n b_n$ for its signed coefficients, RH is
equivalent (through the Laguerre--P\'olya theory and the
Aissen--Schoenberg--Whitney--Edrei characterization
\cite{ASW1952,Edrei1953}) to the assertion that $(\mu_n)$ is a
\emph{P\'olya frequency sequence}: every minor of the one-sided infinite
Toeplitz matrix
\[
  T(i,j) \;=\; \begin{cases} \mu_{i-j}, & j \le i,\\ 0, & j > i,\end{cases}
\]
is nonnegative.  The order-$1$ and order-$2$ fragments of this condition are
classical theorems---positivity of the $\mu_n$ follows from P\'olya's
integral representation \cite{Polya1926}, and the order-$2$ (Tur\'an)
inequalities were proved by Csordas, Norfolk and Varga \cite{CNV1986},
resolving a problem posed by P\'olya \cite{Polya1927}.  Everything at order
$\ge 3$ is open and is, in aggregate, equivalent to RH.

This paper reports a formalization program, carried out in Lean~4 over
Mathlib \cite{Mathlib2020,Lean4}, whose purpose is \emph{not} to prove RH
but to do three things that we believe are individually useful and jointly
new:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prove, with kernel-checked proofs, every step of the
  reduction that is genuinely provable today}, so that the open mathematics
  is isolated into a small number of explicitly named hypotheses
  (\S\ref{sec:method}).  The formal centerpiece is an all-orders Fekete-type
  criterion (\S\ref{sec:fekete}): strict positivity of the \emph{contiguous}
  Toeplitz minors implies the full P\'olya frequency condition.
\item \textbf{Assemble two independent, exact-ready conditional routes to
  RH} (\S\ref{sec:routeA}--\ref{sec:routeB}), each terminating in a Lean
  theorem whose hypotheses are named propositions:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item the kernel/PF route \Lean{riemannHypothesis\_of\_effectiveCertificates},
    whose single open input \Lean{KernelEffectiveCertificates} asks, for each
    matrix order, for a certified finite prefix together with an explicit
    remainder-bound tail; and
  \item the Jensen/HPSS route \Lean{riemannHypothesis\_of\_hpss\_route},
    whose open inputs are the P\'olya--Jensen bridge, the general same-sign
    Hermite--Poulain/Schur--Szeg\H{o} composition theorem, and per-window
    hyperbolicity of the coefficient polynomials.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Prune dead branches by machine-checked falsification}
  (\S\ref{sec:falsification}): we exhibit a kernel-checked counterexample
  showing that the known local Tur\'an data (orders $2$ and $3$) cannot
  close even the order-$3$ contiguous rung at the sequence level, and we
  record why a recent certified $\PF_5$ failure for the continuous
  de Bruijn--Newman kernel \cite{PF5counterexample} forces any proof to
  target the discrete moment sequence directly.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Claims and non-claims.}
No theorem in this paper or in the underlying repository proves RH,
and none assumes it: every RH-equivalent or RH-adjacent statement is carried
as a named \emph{hypothesis} (a Lean \Lean{Prop}), never as an axiom.  An
automated audit (\S\ref{sec:method}) verifies that all 122 audited theorems
depend on nothing beyond Lean's three foundational axioms
(\Lean{propext}, \Lean{Classical.choice}, \Lean{Quot.sound}).  In
particular, we prove (Corollary~\ref{cor:rhstrength}) that our main
positivity hypothesis is \emph{at least RH-strength}, which is exactly why
it must remain a hypothesis.  What the formalization contributes is the
complete, kernel-checked closure of everything between that hypothesis and
RH---a closure which, to our knowledge, had not previously been carried out
in full even informally at this level of granularity.

\section{Background and conventions}\label{sec:background}

We use the completed zeta function
$\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$ and its real form
$\Xi(t) = \xi(\tfrac12 + it)$, an even entire function of order one, real
and positive at $t = 0$.  In the formalization the coefficient sequence is
\emph{defined} derivative-first,
\[
  b_n \;=\; \Lean{XiCoeff}\,n \;=\; \frac{\Xi^{(2n)}(0)}{(2n)!},
  \qquad \mu_n \;=\; (-1)^n b_n ,
\]
so that all downstream theorems are anchored to a concrete real sequence;
the analytic identification of $(b_n)$ with the Taylor coefficients and
P\'olya's kernel representation
$b_n = (-1)^n\,2 M_n/(2n)!$ with $M_n = \int_0^\infty \Phi(u)\,u^{2n}\,du$
and $\Phi > 0$ \cite{Polya1926} are named classical inputs, not silent
assumptions.

For $k, m \in \mathbb{N}$ the \emph{contiguous minor of order $k$ at offset
$m$} is
\[
  D_k(m) \;=\; \det\bigl[\,T(m+i,\,j)\,\bigr]_{0 \le i,j < k},
\]
the determinant of the $k\times k$ block whose rows are consecutive and
whose columns are the initial interval $\{0,\dots,k-1\}$
(\Lean{XiToeplitzContigMinor}).  Three positivity conditions on the family
of \emph{all} minors will occur:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Contiguous, strict:}
    \Lean{XiContigToeplitzStrictPositive}, meaning
    $\forall k,m,\ D_k(m)>0$.
  \item \emph{Initial-column:} \Lean{XiInitialColumnMinorTotalPositive},
    meaning arbitrary rows with columns $0,\ldots,k-1$ and determinant
    $\ge0$.
  \item \emph{Full PF:} \Lean{XiToeplitzTotalPositive}, meaning arbitrary
    rows and columns with determinant $\ge0$.
\end{itemize}
The classical scaffolding is bundled as a structure
\Lean{ClassicalToeplitzScaffolding} with four fields: the
ASW/Edrei bridge from full PF to real-rooted finite approximants of $\Xi$
\cite{ASW1952,Edrei1953}, the Laguerre--P\'olya closure bridge, the
P\'olya--Jensen bridge ($\RH \iff$ hyperbolicity of all Jensen polynomials
\cite{Polya1927}), and the classical converse
($\RH \Rightarrow$ full PF).  Each is a proved theorem of classical
analysis whose formalization is a separate, ongoing program
(\S\ref{sec:newmath}); none is RH-strength.

\section{Formal methodology}\label{sec:method}

The repository comprises 71 Lean modules ($\approx 28{,}000$ lines) over
Mathlib \cite{Mathlib2020}.  Three disciplines are enforced mechanically.

\paragraph{Named hypotheses, never axioms.}  Open mathematics enters only as
a named \Lean{Prop} passed explicitly to conditional theorems.  A source
scanner rejects \Lean{sorry}, \Lean{admit}, and project-level \Lean{axiom}
declarations in the entire library.

\paragraph{Semantic axiom audit.}  A dedicated module runs
\Lean{\#print axioms} on the 122 headline theorems; a CI script fails unless
every one reports exactly
\[
[\Lean{propext},\ \Lean{Classical.choice},\ \Lean{Quot.sound}].
\]
This guards against silent placeholder reintroduction: assuming an
RH-equivalent statement anywhere in a proof would surface immediately.

\paragraph{Falsification before formalization.}  Numerical claims are
treated as falsification evidence, never proof.  Interval-certified scans
(exact rational interval arithmetic over decimal enclosures of the first
$121$ coefficients, indexed $0$ through $120$) test each named positivity target before formal work
begins; certified negative signs would kill a route, and one such
sequence-level route-kill is itself formalized
(\S\ref{sec:falsification}).

\paragraph{Differential numerical replication.}  The bounded minor scan is
implemented independently in Python, JavaScript, and TypeScript.  Python and
TypeScript use exact interval arithmetic under the stated half-ULP
decimal model; JavaScript uses exact midpoint \texttt{BigInt} arithmetic and a
fraction-free Bareiss determinant.  Positive, zero, and negative controls,
the coefficient-file SHA-256 digest, and full sign-stream hashes are compared
automatically.  Agreement strengthens reproducibility of the finite scan but
does not certify the upstream coefficient extraction or any infinite claim.

\section{Synthesis of the formalization program and the literature}
\label{sec:synthesis}

The repository contains more than one proposed route to RH.  A publishable
account must distinguish the common analytic object, the unconditional Lean
algebra, the conditional interfaces, and the exploratory programs that were
rejected or demoted.  This section records that synthesis so that the paper is
not read as claiming that an equivalent reformulation is itself progress on
RH.

\subsection{The corrected object and the common spine}

An early version of the development used the meromorphic completed-zeta
variant $\Lambda_0$.  That is not the correct object for a P\'olya--Jensen or
P\'olya-frequency argument: its critical-line zeros do not coincide with the
non-trivial zeros of $\zeta$.  The current formalization instead defines
\begin{equation}
  \xi(s):=\frac{s(s-1)}{2}\,\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
  \qquad \Xi(t):=\xi\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right),
  \label{eq:xi-common-spine}
\end{equation}
and proves the zero-correspondence theorem
\Lean{Xi\_eq\_zero\_iff\_riemannZeta}.  Every coefficient, Jensen, Toeplitz,
and moment statement in this paper is downstream of this corrected spine.
The correction is a soundness repair, not a claim of a new theorem about
$\zeta$.

\subsection{The Jensen literature and the exact role of Route B}

The Jensen literature is both relevant and cautionary.  The
Griffin--Ono--Rolen--Zagier theorem gives Hermite asymptotics and eventual
fixed-degree information; the Xi-specific effective work makes the asymptotic
regime effective; and O'Sullivan gives a complementary Hermite-combination
criterion and detailed coefficient expansions
\cite{GORZ2019,XiEffective,OSullivan}.  These results motivate the
root-separation and remainder targets in \S\ref{sec:newmath}.

They do not, however, close P\'olya--Jensen.  Farmer's analysis argues that
the Hermite phenomenon can lose the information needed to attack RH and gives
criteria for distinguishing a useful equivalence from an information-losing
one \cite{Farmer2022}.  We incorporate that criticism explicitly: Route B is
an exact formal receptor, not a claim that Jensen asymptotics are by
themselves a plausible proof strategy.  The recent finite-strip analysis of
Planat further sharpens the distinction between eventual large-shift
hyperbolicity and the finite set of low shifts that still carries the RH
content \cite{Planat2026}.  A subsequent experimental study reports
parity-dependent onset behavior, finite Hankel positivity, and Pad\'e evidence
for a conjectural Stieltjes bridge \cite{PlanatStieltjes2026}.  We use these
observations only to motivate falsification targets; no conjectural bridge is
consumed by Route~B.

\subsection{The PF literature and the discrete/continuous separation}

The ASW--Edrei characterization and the Fekete criterion supply the classical
total-positivity context \cite{ASW1952,Edrei1953,GK,Pinkus}.  Katkova applies
the multiply-positive/PF hierarchy directly to the Riemann Xi generating
function and records the relevant consecutive-minor criterion
\cite{Katkova2007}.  Our contribution is the Lean proof of the
Toeplitz-specific algebraic closure and its exact receptors, not a new
classification of PF sequences or discovery of the classical criterion.

A July 2026 preprint gives an explicit uniform tail wedge for the discrete Xi
coefficients: in its normalization, every consecutive minor of order $r\ge2$
is positive once the shift is at least $10^{18}r^3$
\cite{CubicWedge2026}.  The result is recent and has not been independently
rechecked or transported into Lean here, but it directly informs the revised
Route~A frontier below.  By contrast, the certified PF$_5$ failure for the
continuous de Bruijn--Newman kernel \cite{PF5counterexample} is retained as a
route-kill: it prevents silently replacing the discrete factorial-weighted Xi
moments by a continuous PF$_\infty$ kernel.  Any future proof must establish a
discrete transfer theorem or prove the discrete minors directly.

\subsection{Position relative to other formalization projects}

The Lean Millennium Prize Problems repository formalizes the official problem
statements while leaving the RH proof open \cite{LeanMillennium}.  Other active
Lean projects publish conditional RH reductions with named analytic frontiers
\cite{NagyRH,BeanapologistRH}.  The defensible distinction of the present work
is narrower: it provides an axiom-audited Fekete/Toeplitz all-orders closure,
an effective-certificate assembly theorem, a bounded-degree hyperbolic-limit
theorem, and a Schur--Szeg\H{o} receptor, together with falsification artifacts
for the tempting local routes.  We therefore claim a formalized algebraic
closure and an exact-ready interface, not the first or only Lean reduction of
RH.

\section{The Fekete engine}\label{sec:fekete}

Fekete's classical criterion---strict positivity of solid minors implies
strict total positivity \cite{GK,Pinkus}---suggests that the contiguous
ladder should control all minors.  For the one-sided (lower-triangular)
Toeplitz setting we formalize a complete, self-contained version of this
mechanism.  All results in this section are unconditional, kernel-checked
theorems.

\subsection{A generic Grassmann syzygy and three-term identity}

The engine is Xi-free multilinear algebra over an arbitrary commutative
ring $R$ (\Lean{GrassmannSyzygy.lean}).  For $q \ge 0$ let
$W \colon \mathrm{Fin}(q{+}3) \to \mathrm{Fin}(q{+}2) \to R$ be a family of
$q+3$ row vectors in $R^{q+2}$, let $M_i$ denote the maximal minor deleting
row $i$, and for $u < v$ let $S(\widehat{u},\widehat{v})$ denote the
order-$(q{+}1)$ minor on the first $q+1$ columns whose rows are the
ascending complement of $\{u,v\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Grassmann syzygy; \Lean{maxMinor\_syzygy}]\label{thm:syzygy}
For every column index $c$,
$\ \sum_{i=0}^{q+2} (-1)^i\, W_{i,c}\, M_i = 0$.
\end{theorem}

The proof formalizes the classical duplicated-column argument: the
$(q{+}3)\times(q{+}3)$ determinant obtained by adjoining the $c$-th
coordinate column to $W$ vanishes, and Laplace expansion along the adjoined
column yields the syzygy.

\begin{theorem}[Three-term identity; \Lean{grassmann\_three\_term}]\label{thm:threeterm}
For every interior index $0 < t < q+2$,
\[
  M_t \cdot S(\widehat{0},\widehat{q{+}2})
  \;=\;
  M_0 \cdot S(\widehat{t},\widehat{q{+}2})
  \;+\;
  M_{q+2} \cdot S(\widehat{0},\widehat{t}).
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof contracts Theorem~\ref{thm:syzygy} against the cofactor
functional of $q$ fixed spectator rows (killing all but three terms by the
alternating property) and sorts the surviving sub-minors into ascending row
order, with signs computed by the cycle-range permutation
($\Lean{Fin.cycleRange}$, of sign $(-1)^p$).  All signs cancel, yielding
the subtraction-free three-term form.  At orders $3$ and $4$ the identity
specializes to polynomial identities we had previously verified by
\Lean{ring}; Theorem~\ref{thm:threeterm} holds at every order, which is
what the induction below consumes.

\subsection{The all-orders gap-shrinking induction}

\begin{theorem}[All-orders initial-column theorem;
\Lean{xiInitialMinor\_pos\_of\_strictLadder}]\label{thm:allorders}
Assume the strict contiguous ladder
(\Lean{XiContigToeplitzStrictPositive}).  Then for every $k$ and every
strictly increasing row selection $r_0 < \dots < r_{k-1}$, the
initial-column minor
$\det[\,T(r_a, b)\,]_{0\le a,b<k}$ is strictly positive.
\end{theorem}

The proof is a joint induction: an outer induction on the order $k$ (each
order divides by a strict order-$(k{-}1)$ arbitrary-row minor supplied by
the previous stage) and an inner strong induction on the \emph{row
dispersion} $r_{k-1} - r_0 - (k-1)$.  When the dispersion is zero the rows
are consecutive and the minor is a ladder rung.  Otherwise a row $s$ is
inserted into any gap (via \Lean{Fin.insertNth}), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:threeterm}---applied to the extended $(k{+}1)$-row
family---expresses the target minor, times a strictly positive divisor of
order $k-1$, as a nonnegative combination of two order-$k$ minors of
strictly smaller dispersion.  The two ladder rungs $k=1,2$ of the
hypothesis are, respectively, strict positivity of the $\mu_n$ and the
strict Tur\'an inequalities; the formalization records this subsumption
(\Lean{xiMomentStrictTuran\_of\_strictLadder}).

\subsection{Dominance dichotomy, reversal symmetry, and full PF}

\begin{theorem}[Dominance dichotomy;
\Lean{xiMinor\_eq\_zero\_of\_lt}, \Lean{xiMinor\_pos\_of\_dominant}]\label{thm:dichotomy}
Let $r_0 < \dots < r_{k-1}$ and $c_0 < \dots < c_{k-1}$ be arbitrary
strictly increasing row and column selections.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $r_a < c_a$ for some $a$, then
  $\det[\,T(r_a,c_b)\,] = 0$.
\item If $c_a \le r_a$ for all $a$ and the strict contiguous ladder holds,
  then $\det[\,T(r_a,c_b)\,] > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Part (1) is a permutation-pigeonhole argument on the lower-triangular zero
pattern: every permutation term contains an entry above the support.  Part
(2) repeats the gap-shrinking induction with the columns fixed but
arbitrary; its base case (consecutive rows, arbitrary dominated columns)
reduces to Theorem~\ref{thm:allorders} by a Toeplitz \emph{reversal
symmetry} (\Lean{xiMinor\_contigRows}): reversing both index orders and
transposing carries a contiguous-row minor to an arbitrary-row
initial-column minor.  In the induction step the only possibly degenerate
term is the drop-last-row flanking minor, and there part (1) supplies the
required nonnegativity.

\begin{corollary}[Full PF from the strict ladder;
\Lean{xiToeplitzTotalPositive\_of\_strictLadder}]\label{cor:fullpf}
The strict contiguous ladder implies the full P\'olya frequency condition
\Lean{XiToeplitzTotalPositive}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Sharpest reduction, and RH-strength labeling;
\Lean{riemannHypothesis\_of\_strictLadder}]\label{cor:rhstrength}
Given the classical scaffolding, the strict contiguous ladder implies RH.
Since, conversely, the scaffolding makes the full PF condition
\emph{equivalent} to RH
(\Lean{xiToeplitzTotalPositive\_iff\_riemannHypothesis}), the strict ladder
is at least RH-strength: it can never be assumed, axiomatized, or verified
numerically.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:fullpf} eliminates two inputs that earlier versions of
the reduction required: Cryer's lower-triangular initial-column criterion
\cite{Cryer1976} and P\'olya's kernel representation are no longer consumed
by the minimal path (both remain available, as named classical inputs, to
secondary routes).

\section{Route A: effective kernel certificates}\label{sec:routeA}

Under P\'olya's representation the coefficient-side and kernel-side
contiguous minors are related by the exact scaling
$D_k(m) = 2^k \cdot D^{\mathrm{ker}}_k(m)$, where
$D^{\mathrm{ker}}_k$ is built from the factorial-weighted moments
$M_n/(2n)!$; strict positivity therefore transfers verbatim
(\Lean{xiToeplitzContigMinor\_pos\_iff\_moment\_of\_kernelRep}).  This
suggests organizing the open mathematics \emph{order by order}:

\begin{definition}[Per-order effective certificates;
\Lean{KernelEffectiveCertificates}]\label{def:cert}
For a moment model $M$: for every order $k$ there exists a cutoff $N$ with
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{certified prefix} (\Lean{KernelMinorPrefixStrict}):
  $D^{\mathrm{ker}}_k(m) > 0$ for all $m < N$ --- for rational interval
  enclosures of the moments this is dischargeable offset-by-offset by
  \Lean{norm\_num};
\item \emph{explicit remainder tail} (\Lean{KernelMinorTailStrict}):
  $D^{\mathrm{ker}}_k(m) > 0$ for all $m \ge N$ --- the target of effective
  asymptotic analysis.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Route A; \Lean{riemannHypothesis\_of\_effectiveCertificates}]
Classical scaffolding $+$ P\'olya kernel data $+$
\Lean{KernelEffectiveCertificates}\ $\Longrightarrow$ RH.
\end{theorem}

Every interface in this statement is a proved theorem: certificates
assemble into the kernel-side strict ladder, transfer to the
coefficient-side ladder, and feed
Corollary~\ref{cor:rhstrength}.  The order-$3$ instance connects to an
existing prefix/ratio-tail decomposition of the first open rung
(compatibility lemma \Lean{order3TailGap\_of\_kernelTailStrict}); the
current interval scans certify the contiguous order-$3$ prefix through
offset $116$ with smallest normalized value $\approx 4.2\times10^{-6}$.  In
the corrected row box $0\le r_i\le48$, all $18{,}424$ order-$3$ and
$211{,}876$ order-$4$ arbitrary-row initial-column selections are certified
positive, with no negative, zero, or inconclusive intervals.  The complete
row-$48$ scan is now reproduced independently by Python and TypeScript exact
interval implementations and a JavaScript midpoint implementation; the
row-$30$ baseline remains available for regression comparison.  These are
falsification evidence, not proof.

\section{Route B: the Jensen/Schur--Szeg\H{o} receptor}\label{sec:routeB}

The second route runs through P\'olya's equivalence of RH with
hyperbolicity (real-rootedness) of all Jensen polynomials
$J_{d,n}(x) = \sum_{k\le d}\binom{d}{k} b_{n+k} x^k$ \cite{Polya1927}.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-degree hyperbolic limits;
\Lean{polynomialHyperbolic\_of\_tendsto}]\label{thm:hyplimit}
Let $p_j$ be real polynomials of degree at most $D$, each hyperbolic, whose
complexifications converge pointwise on $\mathbb{C}$ to that of a nonzero
real polynomial $f$.  Then $f$ is hyperbolic.
\end{theorem}

The formal proof is elementary and complex-analysis-free: for a real-rooted
polynomial and two points on a horizontal line $\mathrm{im}\,z = y \ne 0$,
each root factor obeys
$\lvert z_2 - r\rvert^2 \le K \lvert z_1 - r\rvert^2$ with
$K = 2 + 2(\mathrm{re}\,z_2 - \mathrm{re}\,z_1)^2/y^2$, so a nonreal zero of
the limit would force vanishing along the entire line.  This is a proved
leg of the Laguerre--P\'olya closure bridge, which the formalization
correspondingly reduces to a sharper named condition
(\Lean{XiJensenApproximationBridge}).

The Jensen polynomial is exactly the Schur--Szeg\H{o} binomial composition
of its coefficient-window polynomial with $(1+X)^d$
(\Lean{JensenPoly\_eq\_schurSzego\_one\_add\_X\_pow}), and the general
same-sign composition theorem \cite{Szego1922,RS} enters as the named
target \Lean{HermitePoulainSchurSzegoTheorem}; its low-degree cases
($d \le 3$), the hyperbolicity-preservation of differentiation, and a
counterexample showing the \emph{unrestricted} composition claim is false
are proved in the repository.  The receptor result discharges the fixed
factor's side conditions once and for all:

\begin{theorem}[Schur--Szeg\H{o} receptor;
\Lean{jensenPoly\_hyperbolic\_of\_hpss},
\Lean{riemannHypothesis\_of\_hpss\_route}]
The polynomial $(1+X)^d$ has degree $d$, is hyperbolic, and has all real
roots of one sign.  Consequently, given
\emph{(i)} the P\'olya--Jensen bridge,
\emph{(ii)} the general same-sign Schur--Szeg\H{o} theorem, and
\emph{(iii)} hyperbolicity with full degree of every coefficient-window
polynomial of $(b_n)$ (\Lean{XiJensenCoeffHyperbolic}),
RH follows.
\end{theorem}

\section{Machine-checked falsification results}\label{sec:falsification}

\begin{theorem}[Local Tur\'an data cannot close order $3$;
\Lean{turan23\_not\_imp\_toeplitz3}]\label{thm:routekill}
There exist positive reals $b_0,\dots,b_4$ satisfying the strict
second-order Tur\'an inequalities and both third-order Tur\'an inequalities
on the window, for which the contiguous $3\times 3$ Toeplitz determinant is
negative.  A witness is $(b_0,\dots,b_4) = (\tfrac1{100},\,1,\,\tfrac54,\,1,\,\tfrac1{100})$,
with Tur\'an margins $\approx 0.68$ and determinant
$\tfrac{45}{64} - \tfrac{31}{25} + \tfrac{79}{8000} \approx -0.53$.
\end{theorem}

Since the second-order inequalities are the CNV theorem \cite{CNV1986} and
the third-order inequalities are the Dimitrov--Lucas theorem
\cite{DimitrovLucas2011}, Theorem~\ref{thm:routekill} shows that the two
strongest known unconditional local results about $(\mu_n)$ cannot, by
sequence-level algebra alone, prove even the first open rung of the ladder:
any proof must use quantitative information that the five-term Tur\'an
window forgets (the $\Xi$ windows have $\mu_m^2/(\mu_{m-1}\mu_{m+1}) \to 1^+$
with specific super-exponential decay, far from the witness's regime).

Separately, a recent computer-certified failure of $\PF_5$ for the
continuous de Bruijn--Newman translation kernel $\Phi(|u|)$
\cite{PF5counterexample} rules out any argument that silently upgrades that
kernel to $\PF_\infty$; it does \emph{not} bear on the discrete moment
sequence targets above, and the repository records this separation so the
two objects cannot be conflated.

\section{The new mathematics required for an RH proof}\label{sec:newmath}

The formal reduction is now short enough that the remaining mathematics can
be stated as explicit proof obligations rather than as a general request for
``more asymptotics.''  This section gives the proposed statements in the
notation consumed by the Lean receptors.  A statement marked \emph{target}
is not claimed as a theorem of the present repository; it is a precise
mathematical problem whose proof would close a named interface.  The first
two conditional capstones below are proved in Lean once their hypotheses are
supplied; the heat-flow item is a proposed third receptor.

\subsection{The order-three tail as an interval theorem}

For a positive coefficient sequence $(\mu_n)$, write
\begin{equation}
  \rho_n := \frac{\mu_{n+1}}{\mu_n},\qquad
  \Delta_3(m):=\det\!\begin{bmatrix}
    \mu_m&\mu_{m-1}&\mu_{m-2}\\
    \mu_{m+1}&\mu_m&\mu_{m-1}\\
    \mu_{m+2}&\mu_{m+1}&\mu_m
  \end{bmatrix},\qquad
  G_3(m):=\frac{\Delta_3(m)}{\mu_m^3}.
  \label{eq:normalized-three-gap}
\end{equation}
For $m\ge2$, direct division by $\mu_m^3$ gives the ratio expression
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
G_3(m)={}&1-2\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_{m-1}}
 +\frac{\rho_m^2}{\rho_{m-2}\rho_{m-1}}
 +\frac{\rho_m\rho_{m+1}}{\rho_{m-1}^2}\\[-0.2em]
&-\frac{\rho_m\rho_{m+1}}{\rho_{m-2}\rho_{m-1}}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:ratio-three-gap}
\end{equation}
This identity is useful because asymptotic results naturally control ratios,
whereas the Lean target is a determinant.

\begin{definition}[Effective ratio box]
An effective ratio box at offset $m$ is a tuple of rational intervals
\begin{equation}
  I_{m,j}=[\underline\rho_{m+j},\overline\rho_{m+j}]
  \quad (j=-2,-1,0,1),
  \qquad 0<\underline\rho_{m+j}\le\overline\rho_{m+j},
  \label{eq:ratio-box}
\end{equation}
containing the four adjacent ratios.  Let $\mathcal G_3(I_{m,-2},\ldots,I_{m,1})$
be the natural interval evaluation of the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:ratio-three-gap}.
\end{definition}

\begin{target}[Effective $k=3$ remainder compiler]
Suppose that, for some explicit $N$, effective asymptotics produce ratio boxes
\eqref{eq:ratio-box} for every $m\ge N$ and that
\begin{equation}
  \inf\mathcal G_3(I_{m,-2},I_{m,-1},I_{m,0},I_{m,1})>0
  \qquad(m\ge N).
  \label{eq:positive-three-tail}
\end{equation}
Then $\Lean{KernelMinorTailStrict}(M,3,N)$ holds.  In particular, the
proof is an exact rational interval calculation once the asymptotic error
terms have been translated into the four intervals.
\end{target}

The target isolates the genuinely new analytic step: one must obtain an
error margin that survives the cancellation in \eqref{eq:ratio-three-gap}.
An asymptotic equivalence without an explicit signed remainder is
insufficient.  O'Sullivan's Lambert-$W$ expansions \cite{OSullivan} are a
natural source of such bounds; the missing work is the remainder translation,
not another Lean interface.

\subsection{From one rung to every order}

The Fekete engine gives the following exact capstone, which is the central
all-order theorem of the formalization.

\begin{theorem}[All-order positivity capstone]
Assume the classical Toeplitz scaffolding and
\begin{equation}
  \forall k,m\in\mathbb N,\qquad D_k(m)>0.
  \label{eq:strict-ladder}
\end{equation}
Then every minor of the signed Xi Toeplitz matrix is nonnegative and RH
holds.  In Lean this is
\Lean{riemannHypothesis\_of\_strictLadder}, obtained through
\Lean{xiToeplitzTotalPositive\_of\_strictLadder}.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is conditional because \eqref{eq:strict-ladder} is itself at
least RH-strength.  Route A packages the order-by-order version of this
obligation: a finite certified prefix plus a tail certificate for each $k$.
Thus an all-order asymptotic theorem need not mention Toeplitz minors at all;
it only has to imply the named proposition
\Lean{KernelEffectiveCertificates}.

The order-three compiler above is the first nontrivial instance.  A recent
preprint now supplies a much stronger coefficient-side statement
\cite{CubicWedge2026}: for every order $k\ge2$,
\begin{equation}
  D_k(m)>0\qquad\text{whenever}\qquad m\ge10^{18}k^3.
  \label{eq:cubic-tail-wedge}
\end{equation}
Its matrix is the transpose of our consecutive block.  In the preprint's
normalization $G(z)=\tfrac18\xi(\tfrac12+\tfrac12\sqrt z)$, its positive
coefficient is $a_n=\mu_n/(8\cdot4^n)$; hence an order-$k$ minor at shift $m$
is multiplied by the positive factor $8^{-k}4^{-km}$, so the determinant
signs agree.  We have not independently rerun
the preprint's Arb ancillary certificates or formalized this transport;
\eqref{eq:cubic-tail-wedge} is therefore cited as an external preprint theorem,
not counted among the 122 audited Lean theorems.

If \eqref{eq:cubic-tail-wedge} is accepted, the former uniform-tail target has
an explicit solution $N(k)=10^{18}k^3$.  The remaining Route~A obligation is
the complementary wedge.

\begin{target}[Complementary finite-prefix wedge]
Certify $D_k(m)>0$ for every $k\ge2$ and every
$0\le m<10^{18}k^3$, in the exact coefficient normalization consumed by the
Lean receptor.  Together with \eqref{eq:cubic-tail-wedge}, these certificates
instantiate the strict contiguous ladder.
\end{target}

The word ``finite'' is per order only: the union over all $k$ remains infinite
and RH-strength.  For order three alone the external cutoff is
$2.7\times10^{19}$, so the current prefix through offset $116$ leaves an
enormous interval.  The preprint changes the shape of the frontier from an
unknown tail to a certified-tail/uncertified-wedge split; it does not make the
remaining verification computationally routine.

\subsection{A root-separation theorem for the Jensen route}

Let $p(x)=a\prod_{i=1}^d(x-r_i)$ be a real polynomial with distinct real
roots, and choose $0<s<\tfrac12\min_{i\ne j}|r_i-r_j|$.  Set
\begin{equation}
  \eta(p,s):=\min_{1\le i\le d}\min_{|z-r_i|=s}|p(z)|>0.
  \label{eq:rouche-margin}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Root-separation perturbation]
Let $q$ be a real polynomial of degree $d$ and suppose
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{|z-r_i|=s}|q(z)-p(z)|<\eta(p,s)
  \qquad(1\le i\le d).
  \label{eq:root-separation-condition}
\end{equation}
Then $q$ has exactly one zero in each disk $|z-r_i|<s$, and every zero of
$q$ is real.  Consequently $q$ is hyperbolic.
\end{theorem}

Indeed, Rouch\'e's theorem gives one zero in each disk.  The disks are
pairwise disjoint and $q$ has degree $d$, so these are all its zeros.  A
nonreal zero in a disk centered on the real axis would bring its conjugate
into the same disk, contradicting the count one.  This theorem is elementary
but exact-ready: coefficient bounds imply the left side of
\eqref{eq:root-separation-condition} by the triangle inequality.

\begin{target}[Xi Jensen window certificate]
For every degree $d$ there are explicit $n_0(d)$, Hermite-model polynomials
$H_d$, and margins $s_d,\eta_d>0$ such that for every $n\ge n_0(d)$ the
normalized coefficient-window polynomial of the Xi coefficients satisfies
\eqref{eq:root-separation-condition} with $p=H_d$.  The finite set of windows
$n<n_0(d)$ is discharged by exact algebra or certified interval arithmetic.
This target implies \Lean{XiJensenCoeffHyperbolic}.
\end{target}

The asymptotic input from Griffin--Ono--Rolen--Zagier \cite{GORZ2019} gives
the Hermite shape; an effective version must additionally bound the root
separation margin and the coefficient error in a common normalization.  This
is a concrete alternative to proving the full same-sign
Hermite--Poulain/Schur--Szeg\H{o} theorem for every degree.

\subsection{Conditional capstones and a possible heat-flow route}

The exact-ready architecture can be summarized as the following theorem
schema.  It is deliberately stated with named propositions, so the proof
does not hide a conjecture in a typeclass or an imported declaration.

\begin{theorem}[Three conditional RH capstones]
Assume the classical analytic scaffolding.  Each of the following hypotheses
is separately sufficient for RH:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \Lean{KernelEffectiveCertificates};
  \item \Lean{PolyaJensenBridge},
    \Lean{HermitePoulainSchurSzegoTheorem}, and
    \Lean{XiJensenCoeffHyperbolic};
  \item a proved heat-flow rigidity proposition
    \Lean{HeatFlowRigidity} asserting that the de Bruijn--Newman constant
    satisfies $\Lambda\le0$.
\end{enumerate}
The first two implications are the Lean theorems
\Lean{riemannHypothesis\_of\_effectiveCertificates} and
\Lean{riemannHypothesis\_of\_hpss\_route}; the third combines
$\Lambda\ge0$ of Rodgers--Tao \cite{RodgersTao2020} with the classical
equivalence $\RH\iff\Lambda\le0$.
\end{theorem}

The third item is a research direction, not a hidden assumption.  A possible
mathematical target is a quantitative backward-heat-flow rigidity statement:
if $\Lambda>0$, then a specified zero-statistics inequality is violated at an
explicit height.  Proving that implication would produce
\Lean{HeatFlowRigidity} and close a third route without Toeplitz positivity.

\subsection{What remains genuinely open}

The four classical bridges (ASW/Edrei, Laguerre--P\'olya closure,
P\'olya--Jensen, and the converse) are formalization work, albeit substantial
work involving entire functions and Hadamard products.  Subject to independent
verification, the cubic-wedge preprint supplies the all-order tail shape
formerly missing from Route~A; what remains is its normalization-exact Lean
transport and the complementary finite-prefix wedge.  The Jensen route still
requires an effective root-separation certificate such as
\eqref{eq:root-separation-condition}.  None of these remaining statements is
currently proved in the repository in the exact Lean form.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Formalized analytic number theory has advanced rapidly---Mathlib contains
the nonvanishing of $\zeta$ on $\mathrm{Re}(s) \ge 1$, the functional
equation, and discreteness of the zero set, while independent Lean projects
now formalize other RH reductions and the official Millennium-problem
statement \cite{LeanMillennium,NagyRH,BeanapologistRH}.  Our narrower claim is
that the present repository combines a generic Grassmann three-term
gap-shrinking identity, a Toeplitz-specific all-orders closure, and two
axiom-audited exact-ready receptors in one checked development.  The
counterexample of Theorem~\ref{thm:routekill} appears to be new as a stated
separation, though it is elementary once asked.

The honest summary is unchanged from the abstract: RH is not proved, and
the remaining inputs are provably at least as hard as RH (the strict
ladder) or are classical formalization debt (the scaffolding).  What has
changed is the shape of the problem: the open mathematics is now a short
list of named statements, each with a kernel-checked theorem waiting to
consume it.  All results are reproducible from the repository by running
\Lean{lake build}, the axiom-audit script, and the verification harness;
the audit reports 122 theorems depending only on Lean's foundational axioms.
