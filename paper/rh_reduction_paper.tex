\begin{abstract}
We present a formally verified study, in Lean~4 / Mathlib, of the
Laguerre--Pólya / total-positivity approach to the Riemann Hypothesis (RH).
\textbf{We do not prove RH.} We prove a chain of \emph{conditional reductions},
each checked by the Lean kernel and depending only on the foundational axioms
$\{\mathsf{propext},\allowbreak \mathsf{Classical.choice},\allowbreak \mathsf{Quot.sound}\}$.
They convert RH into total-positivity statements about an explicit positive kernel and
into an operator-positivity (pseudo-Hermitian) statement. The central formal statement is a
\emph{tightness theorem}. Assume three classical inputs that are not machine-checked
here (Pólya--Jensen, Aissen--Schoenberg--Whitney/Edrei and the Laguerre--Pólya
closure) and the classical converse. Then RH is \emph{equivalent} to total positivity of the
Toeplitz matrix of the sign-normalized $\xi$ Taylor coefficients. The equivalence
itself is classical; it is stated explicitly for $\xi$ by Katkova. Our contribution is a
machine-checked form with every external input named. It makes precise that
the lone open input cannot be ``postulated'' as progress: doing so is logically
identical to assuming RH. Along the way we identify and repair a target-function
error. The error was building the program on Mathlib's entire completed zeta $\Lambda_0$,
whose zeros are \emph{not} the Riemann zeros, rather than on Riemann's $\xi$. We
also record a moment-determinant ladder, a Turán-to-Jensen lemma in degree two,
and an independent pseudo-Hermitian reduction. Numerically, we start from
rigorous FLINT/Arb enclosures of the coefficients $\mu_0,\dots,\mu_{120}$. In ball
arithmetic, we certify that all $1816$ contiguous Toeplitz minors of order $\le16$
in this range are strictly positive. We also certify that $(\mu_n)$ is strictly log-concave for
$n\le118$. These are consistency checks against known theorems, not new
positivity results; the known theorems are Katkova's finite-order Pólya-frequency theorem,
the Csordas--Norfolk--Varga Turán inequalities and a recent proof of infinite
log-concavity. The open content is \emph{uniformity} in the order. This
version updates the June 2026 draft. It certifies the numerics, corrects their
positioning against the literature, and points to an archived, axiom-audited Lean
release.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and scope}

\paragraph{Disclaimer.} This paper is a \emph{formalization and reduction study},
not a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, and it makes no claim to be one. Every
implication labelled ``verified'' is checked mechanically; every classical theorem
we rely upon but do not formalize is cited explicitly as an \emph{external input}
and is never silently assumed. The defensible contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a correct, machine-checked and \emph{tight} formal version of the classical
    reduction of RH to a single sign-correct positivity statement;
  \item the identification and repair of a target-function error that arises
    naturally when working with Mathlib's completed zeta;
  \item an independent operator-positivity reduction;
  \item reproducible, ball-arithmetic consistency checks of the reduction's premise
    in a finite range.
\end{enumerate}
We do not claim that the equivalence, the Turán inequalities or finite-order
Pólya-frequency positivity of the $\xi$ coefficients are new; see
Section~\ref{sec:related}.

\paragraph{Version history and relation to later work.} This manuscript was
drafted in June 2026 (reserved DOI \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.20574510}) and is
released here as version~1.0.0. Version~1.0.0 makes four changes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item it replaces two-precision floating cross-checks with ball-arithmetic
    certification (Section~\ref{sec:numerics});
  \item it removes earlier claims that the computation exceeded what is known;
    Katkova's theorem already yields nonnegativity of every minor of order
    $\le 43$;
  \item it records that frontier item~C of the June draft is settled in the
    literature;
  \item it ties every cited Lean theorem to an archived commit.
\end{enumerate}
The subsequent development is reported separately. A formalized Fekete-type
closure from strict contiguous minors to full Pólya-frequency positivity, together
with effective and Jensen receptor interfaces, appears in~\cite{Mondal2026TwoRoutes}.
An independent prime-defined Weil-form programme appears in~\cite{Mondal2026Weil}.

\paragraph{The object.} Riemann's entire $\xi$ function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi}
  \xi(s) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,s(s-1)\,\pi^{-s/2}\,\Gamma(s/2)\,\zeta(s)
\end{equation}
is entire, and its zeros are exactly the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$; RH asserts
they all lie on $\re s = \tfrac12$. On the critical line, the real even function
$\Xib(t) = \xi(\tfrac12 + it)$ has a Taylor expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:taylor}
  \Xib(t) \;=\; \sum_{n\ge 0} b_n\, t^{2n},\qquad b_n\in\R,
\end{equation}
and we set the \emph{sign-normalized sequence} $\mu_n := (-1)^n b_n$.

\paragraph{Two classical pillars.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Pólya--Jensen}~\cite{Polya1927,CNV1986}: RH is equivalent to
  hyperbolicity (all roots real) of every Jensen polynomial
  $J_{d,n}(X) = \sum_{k=0}^d \binom{d}{k} b_{n+k} X^k$.
  \item \emph{Aissen--Schoenberg--Whitney / Edrei}~\cite{ASW1952,Edrei1953,Karlin1968}:
  a real sequence is a \emph{Pólya-frequency} (PF) sequence---all minors of the
  Toeplitz matrix $[a_{i-j}]$ are nonnegative---iff its generating function is
  $e^{\gamma z}\prod(1+\alpha_i z)/\prod(1-\beta_j z)$ with
  $\gamma,\alpha_i,\beta_j\ge 0$; for an entire generating function this is exactly
  Laguerre--Pólya membership.
\end{itemize}
Together: \emph{RH $\iff (\mu_n)$ is a Pólya-frequency sequence.} This
consequence is classical; Katkova~\cite{Katkova2007} states it explicitly for
$\xi_1(z)=\xi(\tfrac12+\sqrt z)=\sum_n\mu_n z^n$. She also proves that $(\mu_n)$ is a
Pólya-frequency sequence of every finite order up to an explicit bound, using a
zero-free sector theorem of Schoenberg. Her printed proof gives order $43$, while her
statement gives $44$; we use $43$. Here $\mu_n$ coincides with Katkova's
coefficients and with $\lambda_n=\xi^{(2n)}(\tfrac12)/(2n)!$
of~\cite{LiuMao2026}.

\paragraph{Pólya's positive kernel.} By Pólya's representation~\cite{Polya1926},
$\Xib(t) = 2\int_0^\infty \Phi(u)\cos(ut)\,du$ with $\Phi>0$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
  b_n = (-1)^n\,\frac{2 M_n}{(2n)!},\qquad
  M_n := \int_0^\infty \Phi(u)\,u^{2n}\,du > 0,
  \qquad \mu_n = \frac{2 M_n}{(2n)!} > 0 .
\end{equation}

\section{The target-function correction: $\xi$, not $\Lambda_0$}\label{sec:correction}

We record an error and its repair, because catching it is part of the
contribution. Mathlib's \emph{entire} completed zeta
$\Lambda_0=\texttt{completedRiemannZeta}\textsubscript{\texttt{0}}$ satisfies
$\Lambda = \code{completedRiemannZeta} = \Lambda_0 - 1/s - 1/(1-s)$.

\begin{proposition}[Wrong target]\label{prop:wrong}
The zeros of $\Lambda_0$ are not the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$. Indeed, at a
nontrivial zero $\rho$ one has $\Lambda(\rho)=0$ but
$\Lambda_0(\rho) = \tfrac1\rho + \tfrac1{1-\rho}\neq 0$. On the critical line,
$\Lambda_0(\tfrac12+it) = \bigl(1 - 2\,\Xib(t)\bigr)/(t^2+\tfrac14)$, whose zeros
form the level set $\{\Xib = \tfrac12\}$, unrelated to RH.
\end{proposition}

The remedy is to remove the poles of $\Lambda$ by \emph{multiplying} by
$s(s-1)/2$ (which preserves zeros), recovering $\xi$ of~\eqref{eq:xi}. We verify
the corrected slice has the correct zero set:
\begin{theorem}[\code{Xi_eq_zero_iff_riemannZeta}; verified]\label{thm:zeros}
For all $t\in\R$, $\ \Xib(t) = 0 \iff \zeta(\tfrac12 + it) = 0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (mechanized)]
We define $\Xib(t) = -\tfrac{t^2+1/4}{2}\,Z(t)$ where
$Z(t)=\re\Lambda(\tfrac12+it)$ is the verified real critical-line slice. The scalar
$-\tfrac{t^2+1/4}{2}$ is a nonzero real, so $\Xib(t)=0 \iff Z(t)=0 \iff
\Lambda(\tfrac12+it)=0 \iff \zeta(\tfrac12+it)=0$ (the last step using
$\Lambda = \Gamma_{\R}\cdot\zeta$ with $\Gamma_\R(\tfrac12+it)\neq0$). All steps are
checked in Lean.
\end{proof}

All total-positivity, Jensen, and moment results below are stated for this
corrected $\xi$.

\section{Verified reductions}\label{sec:reductions}

Throughout, ``verified'' means the Lean theorem reduces to
$\{\mathsf{propext},\mathsf{Classical.choice},\mathsf{Quot.sound}\}$, confirmed by
\texttt{\#print axioms}. Definitions of type \texttt{Prop} that encode classical
theorems we do not formalize are \emph{named external inputs}; they are never
asserted, only taken as explicit hypotheses.

\subsection{The Pólya-frequency / Toeplitz route}

\begin{definition}[\code{XiToeplitzTotalPositive}]
The sign-normalized sequence $(\mu_n)$ is \emph{Pólya-frequency total positive} if
every minor of the Toeplitz matrix $[\mu_{i-j}]$ (over all strictly monotone row and
column index selections) is nonnegative.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Low-order rungs; verified]\label{prop:rungs}
Under \code{XiToeplitzTotalPositive}: (i) every $1\times1$ minor gives
$\mu_n\ge 0$; (ii) the contiguous $2\times2$ minor equals the Turán determinant,
$\mu_{n+1}^2-\mu_n\mu_{n+2}$, so its nonnegativity is exactly the Turán inequality
$b_n b_{n+2}\le b_{n+1}^2$; (iii) for every order $k$ and offset $m$ the contiguous
minor $\det[\mu_{m+i-j}]_{i,j<k}\ge 0$.
\end{proposition}

We further prove (Lemma \code{xiToeplitzMinor3_sign_eq}) that the order-$3$ signed
minor equals $(-1)^n$ times the unsigned $b$-determinant, exhibiting the correct
alternating-positivity structure; and we document that the Hankel/moment route used
in an earlier draft is the \emph{wrong} machine, since Hankel positivity forces
\emph{log-convexity}, the opposite of the (true) Turán log-concavity.

\subsection{Tightness of the reduction}

Let the three classical external inputs be: \code{XiToeplitzTPToScaledFiniteBridge}
(Edrei--ASW), \code{XiLaguerrePolyaClosureBridge} (LP closure), and
\code{PolyaJensenBridge}; and let \code{XiToeplitzTPFromRH} be the classical
converse (RH $\Rightarrow$ $\xi$ is LP $\Rightarrow$ its coefficients are PF).

\begin{theorem}[\code{xiToeplitzTotalPositive_iff_riemannHypothesis}; verified]\label{thm:tight}
Given the three classical bridges and the classical converse,
\[
  \code{XiToeplitzTotalPositive} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \mathrm{RH}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[The honesty linchpin]
Theorem~\ref{thm:tight} is the classical equivalence~\cite{Polya1927,ASW1952,Edrei1953,Katkova2007}
in machine-checked form. Its value is not mathematical novelty. It mechanically pins down
the one hypothesis that is not classical, and shows that this input is \emph{equivalent} to
RH. Hence declaring it an axiom is logically identical to
\texttt{axiom rh : RiemannHypothesis}: it does not constitute a proof, and
\texttt{\#print axioms} would expose the assumption. A reduction is progress only if
its hypothesis is \emph{weaker than, or independent of}, RH; this one is provably
neither.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The complete moment-determinant ladder}

Under Pólya's representation (the named external input \code{XiMomentKernelRep},
encoding~\eqref{eq:moments}), the PF condition transfers to explicit moment
determinants at \emph{every} order.

\begin{theorem}[\code{xiToeplitzContigMinor_eq_of_kernelRep}; verified]\label{thm:ladder}
For all $k,m\in\N$,
\[
  \det\bigl[\mu_{m+i-j}\bigr]_{i,j<k}
  \;=\; 2^{k}\cdot \det\!\left[\frac{M_{m+i-j}}{(2(m+i-j))!}\right]_{i,j<k}.
\]
Consequently (\code{xiToeplitzContigMinor_nonneg_iff_moment_of_kernelRep})
Pólya-frequency positivity at all orders is equivalent to positivity of the
factorial-weighted moment determinants of the explicit positive kernel $\Phi$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (mechanized)]
Each entry of the signed Toeplitz matrix equals $2$ times the corresponding
factorial-weighted moment entry (by~\eqref{eq:moments}); the determinant is then
$2^k$ times the moment determinant by multilinearity (\texttt{Matrix.det\_smul},
$\#\,\mathrm{Fin}\,k=k$). Positivity of $2^k$ gives the equivalence.
\end{proof}

The order-$2$ mechanism is quantified by the \emph{slack factor}
$s_n = \dfrac{(2n+3)(2n+4)}{(2n+1)(2n+2)}$, for which we verify $s_n>1$
(\code{one_lt_slackFactor}) and $s_{n+1}<s_n$ (\code{slackFactor_succ_lt}); thus
$s_n\downarrow 1$.

\subsection{Order-2 unification: Turán $\Rightarrow$ Jensen hyperbolicity}

\begin{theorem}[\code{jensenPoly_two_hyperbolic_of_turan}; verified]\label{thm:jensen2}
If $b_{n+2}\neq 0$ and the Turán inequality $b_n b_{n+2}\le b_{n+1}^2$ holds, then
the degree-$2$ Jensen polynomial $J_{2,n}$ is hyperbolic (all complex roots real).
Hence (\code{allJensenTwo_hyperbolic_of_turan2All}) if all coefficients are nonzero
and all Turán inequalities hold, every $J_{2,n}$ is hyperbolic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (mechanized)]
$J_{2,n}$ maps to $b_{n+2}z^2 + 2b_{n+1}z + b_n$ over $\C$. Completing the square,
$(b_{n+2}z + b_{n+1})^2 = b_{n+1}^2 - b_n b_{n+2} =: D \ge 0$ at any root. The
auxiliary lemma \code{im_zero_of_sq_eq_nonneg_real} ($w^2 = D\in\R_{\ge0}
\Rightarrow \im w = 0$) forces $b_{n+2}z + b_{n+1}\in\R$, hence (as $b_{n+2}\neq0$
real) $\im z = 0$. No square roots and no zero-locus input are used.
\end{proof}
Nondegeneracy $b_{n+2}\neq0$ is supplied by~\eqref{eq:moments} ($M_n>0$).
The analytic input is classical. Csordas, Norfolk and Varga~\cite{CNV1986} proved
Pólya's conjectured Turán inequalities for the $\xi$ coefficients. Dimitrov and
Lucas~\cite{DimitrovLucas2011} proved the higher-order (degree-$3$) Turán
inequalities without assuming RH. Theorem~\ref{thm:jensen2} is the formal bridge
from such inequalities to hyperbolicity, not a new inequality.

\subsection{An independent operator-positivity route}

The naive Hilbert--Pólya operator must be self-adjoint for the ambient inner
product---the obstruction. The pseudo-Hermitian viewpoint~\cite{Mostafazadeh2002,BBM2017}
keeps only a \emph{positive metric}.

\begin{definition}[\code{PseudoHermitianForm}]
For a $\C$-linear $T$ on a $\C$-vector space $E$, a pseudo-Hermitian form is a map
$\eta\colon E\times E\to\C$, linear in the first slot and conjugate-linear in the
second, with $\eta(Tx,y)=\eta(x,Ty)$ (\,$\eta$-self-adjointness\,) and
$\eta(x,x)\neq0$ for $x\neq0$ (\,nondegeneracy, supplied by positive-definiteness\,).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\code{PseudoHermitianForm.eigenvalue_conj_eq} and
\code{riemannHypothesis_of_pseudoHermitianWitness}; verified]\label{thm:ph}
An $\eta$-self-adjoint operator has real eigenvalues. Consequently, if the
critical-strip zeros are realized as the $\eta$-self-adjoint spectrum via the
encoding $\mu(s) = -i\,(s-\tfrac12)$ (real iff $\re s=\tfrac12$), then RH holds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (mechanized)]
For an eigenpair $Tv=\mu v$, $v\neq0$:
$\mu\,\eta(v,v)=\eta(Tv,v)=\eta(v,Tv)=\bar\mu\,\eta(v,v)$, and $\eta(v,v)\neq0$, so
$\mu=\bar\mu$. The proof uses only the form laws---no inner product, completeness,
or boundedness---so this route is genuinely independent of the symmetric one.
Existence of $(E,T,\eta)$ with positive $\eta$ and the prescribed spectrum is open.
\end{proof}

\section{Numerical consistency checks}\label{sec:numerics}

\paragraph{Coefficient enclosures.} The coefficients $\mu_0,\dots,\mu_{120}$ are
enclosed by rigorous balls computed with FLINT/Arb~\cite{Johansson2017}
(python-flint~0.8.0, $4096$ working bits). The computation uses series for the
special functions in $\xi$, with explicit analytic remainders. Every enclosure has relative
accuracy of at least $2317$ bits and exact rational endpoints. The first nine
coefficients agree with an independent theta-integral enclosure. Data and generator are in
\code{research/certified_xi/coefficients_120.json} (SHA-256
\texttt{adf3418d\ldots63b11}) of the archived release. The June draft instead used two
floating-point Cauchy-contour computations ($620$ and $520$ digits), which agree
to $\ge236$ digits. That is a consistency check, not an enclosure, and is superseded here.

\paragraph{Certified claims.} The script \code{paper/certify_reduction_minors.py}
works at $8192$ bits. It certifies in ball arithmetic, so that each output ball lies
strictly in $(0,\infty)$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] $\mu_n>0$ for $0\le n\le120$;
  \item[(ii)] strict log-concavity $\mu_{n+1}^2>\mu_n\mu_{n+2}$ for $0\le n\le118$;
  \item[(iii)] every contiguous zero-extended Toeplitz minor
  $\det[\mu_{m+i-j}]_{i,j<k}$ with $1\le k\le16$ and $m+k-1\le120$ is strictly
  positive. These are $1816$ minors, and the largest relative radius of a determinant
  ball is $6.5\times10^{-669}$, at $k=16$, $m=105$.
\end{enumerate}
The certificate output, including the input hash, is stored in
\code{paper/rh_reduction_minor_certificate.json}. The trust boundary is FLINT/Arb
together with the classical identities behind the enclosures. These are not Lean
certificates. Claim~(iv) of the June draft, Jensen hyperbolicity for $d\le6$ and
$n\le114$, was a floating-point root computation and remains uncertified. We report
it only as a floating-point observation.

\paragraph{What the checks do and do not show.} Nonnegativity in (iii) is already a
theorem: Katkova's finite-order result covers every minor of order $\le43$, at
every index~\cite{Katkova2007}. Claim~(ii) is a special case of the infinite
log-concavity of $(\mu_n)$ proved in~\cite{LiuMao2026}. That proof is a preprint and is
computer-assisted. For Jensen polynomials, hyperbolicity is known for $d\le3$ at
every shift~\cite{CNV1986,DimitrovLucas2011}, and for every fixed $d$ at all
sufficiently large shifts~\cite{GORZ2019,GORTTW}. The computations above are
therefore calibration of conventions and margins: strictness with explicit
error control in a finite range. They are not discoveries of previously unknown
positivity. The figures below were produced in June from the floating-point

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The reduction's premise holds for $\xi$ at every tested
order (Fig.~\ref{fig:minors}), with comfortable low-order margins
(Fig.~\ref{fig:turan}). As the known theorems predict, $\mu$ is log-concave, which is
strictly stronger than the slack-relaxed Turán requirement. Any fixed low rung is
settled. The difficulty is \emph{uniformity}: behaviour as $k\to\infty$, together with
the shifts not covered by~\cite{Michalowski2026wedge}.

\section{The open frontier}\label{sec:frontier}

The reductions convert ``zeros on a line'' into positivity statements about the
explicit positive kernel $\Phi$ and an explicit operator metric---objects amenable
to tools foreign to zero-counting.
\begin{description}
  \item[A. Uniform total positivity.] Prove $\det[\mu_{m+i-j}]\ge0$ for all $k,m$.
  This is RH-equivalent, by Theorem~\ref{thm:tight}. Every \emph{fixed} order up to
  $43$ is classical~\cite{Katkova2007}. A recent preprint claims strict positivity
  of every contiguous minor of order $r\ge2$ at all shifts $m\ge10^{18}r^3$, using
  certified saddle-point estimates~\cite{Michalowski2026wedge}. The open content is
  therefore the complementary region of unbounded order and moderate shift. The continuous
  de~Bruijn--Newman kernel is not PF$_5$~\cite{Michalowski2026pf5}, so a
  continuous-kernel total-positivity shortcut is unavailable. Classical tools are
  Karlin total positivity~\cite{Karlin1968}, Schoenberg Pólya-frequency functions, and
  the variation-diminishing property of the kernel in
  $\sum\mu_n z^n = \int_0^\infty \Phi(u)\cosh(u\sqrt z)\,du$.
  \item[B. Asymptotic margin.] Control $R_n-1$ versus $1-1/s_n\sim 8/(2n)^2$.
  Tools: the Griffin--Ono--Rolen--Zagier Hermite asymptotics~\cite{GORZ2019} and
  their effective form~\cite{GORTTW}. Farmer argues that Jensen-polynomial
  asymptotics alone are not a plausible route to RH~\cite{Farmer2022}.
  \item[C. $\mu$ log-concavity (settled).] The June draft listed
  $\mu_{n+1}^2>\mu_n\mu_{n+2}$ as open. By~\eqref{eq:moments} it is equivalent to
  $M_{n+1}^2/(M_nM_{n+2})>1/s_n=\frac{(2n+1)(2n+2)}{(2n+3)(2n+4)}$. The Turán
  inequalities of Csordas--Norfolk--Varga~\cite{CNV1986} give
  $M_{n+1}^2>\frac{2n+1}{2n+3}M_nM_{n+2}$, which is stronger. A recent preprint proves much more,
  strict \emph{infinite} log-concavity of $(\mu_n)$~\cite{LiuMao2026}. Like
  Jensen asymptotics, this is a consequence of RH and does not imply it.
  \item[D. Operator existence.] Construct $(E,T,\eta)$ with a positive metric and
  spectrum $\{\gamma_k\}$ (operator theory; cf.~\cite{BBM2017}).
\end{description}
The de Bruijn--Newman programme provides an independent dynamical handle on the
same object. It is known that $\Lambda\ge0$~\cite{RodgersTao2020} and
$\Lambda\le0.2$~\cite{Polymath2019}, while RH $\iff\Lambda\le0$.

\begin{remark}[A conditional finite-order improvement]\label{rem:qrh}
Katkova's order $43$ uses two inputs. The first is that nontrivial zeros satisfy
$|\re\rho-\tfrac12|\le\tfrac12$. The second is that no zero has
$|\im\rho|<\gamma_1=14.1347\ldots$. In the variable $z=(s-\tfrac12)^2$, a zero
$\tfrac12+x+i\gamma$ lies at angle $2\arctan(|x|/\gamma)$ from the negative axis.
Schoenberg's sector theorem yields PF$_m$ when this angle is at most $\pi/(m+1)$.
A recent manuscript claims that $\zeta(s)\neq0$ for $\re s>7/8$~\cite{OpenAI2026QRH}.
Its statement is machine-checked in an external Lean library against Mathlib's
\code{riemannZeta}, but it has not yet been refereed. If the claim holds, then
$|x|\le\tfrac38$, the maximal angle drops from $0.07072$ to $0.05305$, and the
same argument gives PF$_{58}$ in place of PF$_{43}$. This is a constant improvement only.
Every fixed order already follows from RH verified to a sufficient height, so fixed
order is not RH-strength. The remark is not used elsewhere in this paper.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Named external inputs (never asserted).}
\code{PolyaJensenBridge}, \code{XiToeplitzTPToScaledFiniteBridge},
\code{XiLaguerrePolyaClosureBridge}, \code{XiMomentKernelRep},
\code{XiToeplitzTPFromRH}. Each is a standard classical theorem; formalizing them in
Mathlib is future work.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item No proof of RH: every RH-implying statement is conditional on an input that
  is RH-equivalent (Theorem~\ref{thm:tight}) or an unformalized classical theorem.
  \item The numerics are finite. Ball arithmetic certifies positivity only for $n\le120$
  and Toeplitz order $k\le16$, under the FLINT/Arb trust boundary. The Jensen
  observations for $d\le6$ are floating-point only. None of this can establish the
  uniform ($k,n\to\infty$) statement that is RH-equivalent, and the certified range
  lies inside what known theorems already cover.
  \item The pseudo-Hermitian existence claim is entirely open and unevidenced.
  \item Equivalence-chain reformulations (e.g.\ ``$\le 1$ zero per height'') are
  correct but RH-equivalent, hence reorganization rather than progress.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Classical background.} Classical sources are Pólya~\cite{Polya1926,Polya1927},
Aissen--Schoenberg--Whitney~\cite{ASW1952}, Edrei~\cite{Edrei1953} and Karlin~\cite{Karlin1968}.
For the $\xi$ coefficients specifically, Katkova~\cite{Katkova2007} gives the
Pólya-frequency formulation and finite-order positivity. The Turán and higher-order Turán
inequalities are due to Csordas--Norfolk--Varga~\cite{CNV1986} and
Dimitrov--Lucas~\cite{DimitrovLucas2011}.

\paragraph{Jensen polynomials and heat flow.} Jensen-polynomial asymptotics are developed
in~\cite{GORZ2019,GORTTW}, with the critique of~\cite{Farmer2022}. On the de~Bruijn--Newman
constant see~\cite{RodgersTao2020,Polymath2019}.

\paragraph{Recent work on the same coefficients.} Three 2026 preprints concern exactly
the coefficients studied here: a tail wedge for contiguous Toeplitz minors~\cite{Michalowski2026wedge},
certified failure of PF$_5$ for the continuous kernel~\cite{Michalowski2026pf5}, and infinite
log-concavity~\cite{LiuMao2026}. A claimed zero-free half-plane $\re s>7/8$~\cite{OpenAI2026QRH}
bears on finite-order constants only (Remark~\ref{rem:qrh}).

\paragraph{Operators and formalization.} For the pseudo-Hermitian viewpoint see
Mostafazadeh~\cite{Mostafazadeh2002} and Bender--Brody--Müller~\cite{BBM2017}. The formal
foundations are Mathlib~\cite{Mathlib2020} and its zeta and $L$-function
library~\cite{LoefflerStoll2025}. Spectral and adelic perspectives (Berry--Keating, Connes)
and the Weil and Li positivity criteria are classical antecedents of the operator and
positivity viewpoints used here.

\section*{Acknowledgement of limitations (summary)}
The verified contribution is a correct, tight, axiom-clean \emph{reduction} of RH
to an explicit positivity statement, plus an independent operator reduction, a
